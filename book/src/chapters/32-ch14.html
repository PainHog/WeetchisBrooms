Chapter 14

Episode 1: Night Market

Storyteller only

This chapter is for the Storyteller. Players shouldn’t read it before they play it: the fun is in finding out.

A demon called the Quiet Man is stealing voices across Larkhaven. He has taken six. He needs seven to sing a door open, and the seventh he wants is the most famous voice in the city. This is an Easy episode, built for a first night.

The opening

Read this aloud, then ask the players where their witches are standing.

Friday night in Old Town. The night market fills the lanes around St Bride’s market hall: frying onions, fairy lights, a hundred voices at once. On the steps of the hall, Bea Santos tunes her guitar, as she does every week, and the crowd goes quiet for her first song. She smiles. She opens her mouth. Nothing comes out. Not a note, not a breath. Her hands fly to her throat. At the edge of the crowd, a man in a grey suit tips his hat and walks away into the fog, and the only ones who see him go are you.

Then tell each witch what their Life wants from them this episode (the Life calls table below).

The innocent: Grace Ellery

Grace hosts the late show on Radio Larkhaven, every weeknight from ten till two. Her voice is famous across the city: warm, low and easy to trust. Taxi drivers, night nurses and insomniacs all know it. She grew up on the Docks, lives in a flat on the Harbour, and is brash, kind and more frightened than she lets on.

Next Friday, Grace is broadcasting live from the night market. That is where the Quiet Man will take her if Doom reaches the Showdown mark.

The threat: the Quiet Man

His stat block is in Chapter 10. He is a demon in a grey suit who steals voices. He never uses his own voice: he speaks in the voices he has stolen. He catches each one in an old glass radio valve that glows faintly, and hangs it at the top of his tower.

  • Plan: seven stolen voices to sing a door open. He has six after the opening.
  • Weakness: his own name spoken aloud, and salt. His name is Ambrose Lisk.
  • Hidden place: the old radio tower in the Heights.
  • Public place: the Friday night market.
  • Signature move (on a 6): Hush: attack two different characters; the first one he hurts can’t cast on their next action. If the innocent is there and free, he goes for her instead.

Go to him, or wait?

At the end of each scene, Doom +1. If Doom has reached 5, it’s too late: the Quiet Man comes to the coven at the night market, with Grace in his grip. If not, and the coven has half the Clues, it knows what he is and where he hides, and may go to the radio tower (this takes no scene). Grace isn’t there.

Leads

Here are six leads to offer. Each says where it happens, the roll, and which Powers fit. Rolls are against the Lead Difficulty (1). A success gives 1 Clue, a Critical 2.

  • Use the What and where answer while the coven has fewer than half the Clues. Use the Weakness answer once it has half or more.
  • Players can come back to a lead. Show them something new there each time.
  • When the coven has all the Clues, tell them the whole Weakness plainly, whichever lead gave the last one.
  • Out in the city, a cost is Exposure +1 if the witch was casting (a Power or Reveal), or else 1 Hurt. Anyone else the coven talks to is a talking lead too.

1. Bea Santos (talking)

Where: her flat over the chip shop on Cooper’s Lane, Old Town. Roll: Heart. Powers that fit: Sight, Voice, Wild.

What and where: Bea can’t speak, but she writes fast. A quiet man asked her for “one song, just for me” and paid her with an old silver coin stamped with a radio mast. When she sang, her voice came out of her as a little light and went into a glass valve in his hand. The coin is from Radio Larkhaven’s first year, 1926.

Weakness: He wouldn’t come near the chip-shop counter. He walked the long way round a spilled salt cellar, and the salt hissed where his shadow touched it.

2. Grace Ellery (talking)

Where: the Radio Larkhaven studio in Old Town, after her show. Roll: Heart. Powers that fit: Sight, Voice, Wild.

What and where: For a week, a caller has rung her phone-in every night and said nothing. Behind the silence, very faintly, five or six voices sing together. Radio Larkhaven began in 1926 in the old radio tower in the Heights. The station still owns the tower and keeps it locked.

Weakness: Grace goes pale and tells the truth: twenty years ago a man in a grey suit offered her a voice people would stop and listen to. She signed his card. She still has it, in a drawer at the studio. It says A. Lisk. (This is the twist; see below.)

3. The market lanes (searching)

Where: the night market after closing, around St Bride’s market hall. Roll: Wits. Powers that fit: Sight, Veil, Wild.

What and where: Every radio on every stall died the moment Bea went silent. Each one is stuck on the same old wavelength, marked on the dials as “Larkhaven Tower”. A trail of grey threads from his suit leads to the bus stop for the Heights.

Weakness: At the salt-fish stall, a ring of coarse salt on the cobbles is still unbroken. Inside it, the last few notes of Bea’s song still hang in the air. He couldn’t step in to take them.

4. The voice clinic (searching)

Where: the files of the voice clinic at Larkhaven General. A nurse’s Life might fit here. Roll: Wits. Powers that fit: Sight, Veil, Wild.

What and where: Five patients this month lost their voices overnight, with no cause anyone can find. Put their addresses on a map and they ring the Heights. Every one of them can see the old radio tower from their bedroom window.

Weakness: One patient, a fish seller from the Harbour, can still whisper. Her notes say she threw a fistful of salt at “the grey man”, and he dropped the valve before he could close it.

5. The Book (research at home)

Where: the attic of the Lantern House. Roll: Wits with +1 die (the Book), with no Exposure; one character per scene. Powers that fit: Sight.

What and where: A page in an old hand: Hush-men, demons who gather voices, always seven, to sing a door open. They nest high up, in places where voices were once sent out into the air.

Weakness: Salt stops its mouth. Speak its true name aloud and it comes undone. It hides its name where it first sang. The coven will have to find the name in the city.

6. The steps of St Bride’s (Reveal)

Where: the steps of the market hall, where Bea sang. Nothing ordinary shows here, so Heart and Wits find nothing. Roll: Reveal (Craft, 1 Magic); a success gives 1 more Clue. It is casting, so a cost is Exposure +1. Powers that fit: Sight.

What and where: A fine silver thread, what is left of Bea’s voice, runs from the steps up over the roofs, north to the Heights. It ends at the top of the old radio tower.

Weakness: The thread hums one word, over and over, in Bea’s voice: the name he made her sing. Lisk. Where it passes over the salt bins at the fish stall, it frays.

Life calls

The hidden place: the old radio tower

A fenced hilltop above the big houses of the Heights, next to the observatory. The coven goes to it with half the Clues; going takes no scene. Nothing here needs a roll to get past. If the coven retreats, everyone leaves together by any way out. Grace isn’t here.

  • The lane and the gate. A steep lane ends at an iron gate with a rusted chain and a sign: Radio Larkhaven. Private. A chain-link fence runs round the whole hilltop.
  • The transmitter hut. A brick hut with boarded windows. Inside: racks of dead valves, a desk, a 1926 logbook, and thick cables running out and up the tower.
  • The yard. Gravel and nettles round the tower’s four concrete feet. Steel guy wires hum in the wind, though there is no wind.
  • The tower. A steel lattice mast with a caged ladder, a platform halfway up, and a platform at the top. Six glowing valves hang from the old aerial up there, wired together. This is where he sings.
  • The cable trench. A covered trench runs from the hut, under the fence, into the observatory grounds.

Ways in: the front gate (the chain is rusted through); a gap in the fence by the observatory path; the cable trench from the observatory grounds. Ways out: the same three, and the steep bank down into the gardens of the big houses (too steep to climb up, easy to slide down).

The Quiet Man waits at the top of the tower, then steps off the platform and floats down to meet the coven, hat in hand.

The public place: the Friday night market

If Doom reaches the Showdown mark, it is Friday again, and the market is packed. In a public place, every round in which anyone was casting raises Exposure by 1 (once per round). Nothing here needs a roll to get past.

  • The Market Street arch. A striped arch at the bottom of the hill, the busiest way in.
  • The lanes of stalls. Food, records, second-hand clothes and fortune-tellers, under strings of fairy lights.
  • St Bride’s steps. Grace’s broadcast desk is set up here, with speakers playing her show to the whole market.
  • St Bride’s market hall. The covered hall with its iron roof, full of stalls, with side doors onto Bell Street.
  • The food yard. Long tables, a small stage and the salt-fish stall.
  • The Harbour Steps. A stone stair from the food yard down to the harbour road.
  • Cooper’s Lane. A narrow back alley behind the chip shop.

Ways in and out: the Market Street arch; the Harbour Steps; Cooper’s Lane; through the market hall to Bell Street.

In his grip: the Quiet Man stands behind Grace at her desk with one grey hand on her shoulder. When she tries to speak into the microphone, nothing comes out of her, and her voice comes out of him. To free her, roll Nerve against Defence; only a success frees her. If she is still in his grip at the end of the first round, she is lost. Once she is free, Rook (or the coven’s call) can Carry her to a place it knows, such as the Lantern House.

The twist

Grace’s famous voice isn’t hers. Twenty years ago she was a shy girl with a thin, quiet voice who dreamed of radio. A polite man in a grey suit offered her a voice people would stop and listen to, on loan. She signed his card. The voice she has is his own: that is why he never speaks with it. He needs it back to finish his song.

The twist comes out on Grace’s lead, or in the showdown, when the Quiet Man speaks for the first time in Grace’s voice. Grace is ashamed and afraid. She may beg the coven to keep her secret, or try to bargain with him herself. Let the players decide what to tell her and what to tell anyone else. The twist changes no rules.

Endings

Win (he is destroyed, and Grace is safe). Lisk comes undone like a suit with the stitches pulled. The valves crack, and six stolen voices fly home over the roofs like sparks. Grace keeps the voice: with its owner gone, it is hers now. Next Friday, Bea sings again.

Partial (he fled, or he was destroyed but Grace was lost). If he fled, he climbs back to the tower with the voices he has and waits to collect the rest. The stolen voices stay stolen. If he was destroyed but Grace was lost, the six voices come home, but Grace went through the half-open door with him. Radio Larkhaven plays her old shows every night, and the city waits for her to come back.

Loss (Doom filled, every witch Down, or the coven retreated). If Doom filled, his plan is complete: Grace is lost and he escapes. On the seventh night, every radio in Larkhaven plays seven voices singing, and above the Heights a door opens in the air. If every witch went Down or the coven retreated, he walks away into the fog, polite as ever. If Grace was still in the fight, she goes with him and is lost. He will be back.

Seen (the result drops one step; a Loss stays a Loss). By morning, a video from the market is everywhere: people with fire in their hands and a man in grey who floats. Or, if the coven was Seen during the investigation, a photo of a witch casting on Cooper’s Lane is on the front of the local paper. Someone at Radio Larkhaven wants to interview them.

Carry-over. If he was destroyed, the Quiet Man gets a page in the Book (next time, start with half the Clues). If he wasn’t, he comes back later, when the Storyteller chooses, with 2 more Toughness each time. A witch who is Down when the coven loses (except by retreating) gets a Scar: 1 Hurt at the start of the next episode.

Closing scene

A week later, the night market is back. Give each player a short Life scene: who noticed they were missing this week, and what it cost them. Then end at the market. If Bea has her voice back, she sings on the steps. Grace’s show plays from every stall. Near midnight she says, “This one’s for whoever was at the market last Friday. You know who you are.” Back at the Lantern House, the lamp over the door is still burning.
