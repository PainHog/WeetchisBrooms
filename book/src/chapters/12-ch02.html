Chapter 2

The Roll

Talking a witness round, searching a burned house, throwing a demon across a room, keeping your feet when it hits back: almost everything in the game uses the same roll. Learn it once and you know how to play.

Rolling

Pick the rating that fits what you’re doing: Heart, Wits, Nerve or Craft (Chapter 3). Roll that many six-sided dice. Each 4, 5 or 6 is a success. Count them.

The Storyteller sets the Difficulty: the number of successes you need. It is usually 1–4. The Unmaking can be higher. No Difficulty is ever below 1: if something would lower it to 0, it stays at 1. Most Difficulties come straight from the episode’s numbers (Chapter 6): leads and brewing use the Lead Difficulty, attacks use the threat’s Defence, and defending uses its Danger. Shelter and Hide are Difficulty 2.

Reading the roll

Success: your successes are at least the Difficulty. It works.

Critical: a Success that shows two or more 6s. It works, and does one thing extra.

Partial: at least half the Difficulty (round up), but less than all of it. It works, with a cost. At Difficulty 1 there is no Partial.

Failure: fewer successes than that. It doesn’t work, and there is a cost.

Criticals

A Critical does one thing extra: +1 damage, +1 Clue, +1 potion or −1 more Exposure, whichever fits. If none fits, you get +1 die on your next roll this episode, inside the +2 limit (below).

Some rules already give a number for a Critical: a lead’s 2 Clues, an attack’s 3 damage, a brew’s three potions. That number already includes the extra.

Costs

A Partial or a Failure comes with a cost. What it is depends on where you are and what you’re doing.

The cost

In a showdown: 1 Hurt.

Anywhere else: Exposure +1 if you were casting out in the city; otherwise 1 Hurt.

So at home in the Lantern House, a cost is always 1 Hurt. Out in the city, an ordinary roll risks Hurt and a casting roll risks Exposure. Hurt is in Chapter 3; Exposure is in Chapter 6.

Casting

Casting means using a Power, a spell, a potion or a guardian gift. It matters in two places. Out in the city, a casting roll’s cost is Exposure. In a public showdown, every round in which anyone was casting raises Exposure (Chapter 7).

  • A helper’s die doesn’t make your roll casting. Neither does the guardian’s Sense.
  • The guardian’s Hold isn’t casting: it costs no Ward and uses no magic.
  • Casting Hide never raises Exposure, so its cost is always 1 Hurt.

Extra dice

Your Power adds 2 dice when it fits what you’re doing. Chapter 3 says what each Power fits. You may leave your Power off a roll. Then your Power doesn’t make the roll casting (a spell or potion still does).

On top of your Power, you can add at most +2 dice from everything else together:

Helpers

To help, spend 1 Magic (the guardian spends 1 Ward) and add 1 die to someone else’s roll. Say how you help in the story. You must be able to help: be there with them, or reach them, such as a phone call during a talking lead or a page read out from the Book during research. The Storyteller says whether you can. Helping takes no action, so anyone can help, even after they have acted, and even while Down. (A Down guardian is the one exception: it has faded back to the home, Chapter 4.)

Your Life, the Book and Sense

Your Life adds 1 die when your job or role fits the roll and the Storyteller agrees: Dana the nurse asking about patients at Larkhaven General, or Tess the bus driver checking the depot’s cameras. The Book adds 1 die when you research it at home (Chapter 5). Sense is a guardian gift that adds 1 die to someone else’s lead, once a scene, outside the +2 limit (Chapter 4).

Leaving your Power off

More dice is usually better, but casting has a price. Out in the city, a casting roll’s cost is Exposure; with your Power left off, an ordinary roll costs 1 Hurt instead. In a public showdown, a round with no casting at all doesn’t raise Exposure. When Exposure is close to full, leaving your Power off can be the smart move.

Group checks

When everyone must do the same thing, such as wading out before the tide covers the drowned village, the Storyteller calls a group check. Everyone rolls. It isn’t an action, so nobody uses their action on it. There is only one cost, and whoever rolled worst takes it.

Two things stand out. Each extra die helps a lot. And at Difficulty 3 or 4, a Partial is common: plan for the cost.

Example: a Critical

In a Standard episode, June Takeda searches the newspaper archive in the University Hill library for old fires. That’s a searching lead: Wits 3. Her Power, Sight, fits leads, so she adds 2 dice. Dana helps by spending 1 Magic, so June rolls 6 dice against the Lead Difficulty of 2. Using Sight makes this a casting roll out in the city, so a short roll would cost Exposure.

She rolls 6, 6, 5, 2, 1, 1. That’s three successes, so it’s a Success, and it shows two 6s, so it’s a Critical. A lead’s Critical gives 2 Clues. No cost.

Example: leaving the Power off

In a Hard episode, the coven’s Exposure is at 3 of its 4 boxes. Tess Lindqvist wants to check the bus depot’s cameras for the people who walked into the sea: a searching lead, Wits 2, against Difficulty 2. Her Power, Veil, fits searching leads. But with Veil on, any Partial or Failure would cost Exposure, and the coven would be Seen.

So Tess leaves Veil off. She is a bus driver, and the Storyteller agrees her Life fits: +1 die. She spends 1 Magic for another: +2 in all, the limit. She rolls 4 dice: 5, 3, 2, 1. One success is a Partial. She gets 1 Clue, and because she wasn’t casting, the cost is 1 Hurt instead of Exposure. Tess marks 1 Hurt, and the coven stays unseen.

Example: a group check

The tide is coming in over the drowned village. Dana, June and Tess must all wade out: a group check, Nerve against Difficulty 2. Nobody is casting. Dana rolls 4, 4, 2: a Success. Tess rolls 6, 3, 1: a Partial. June, with Nerve 1, rolls a 2: a Failure. There is one cost, and June rolled worst, so she marks 1 Hurt.
