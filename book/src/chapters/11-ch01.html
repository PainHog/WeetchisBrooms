Chapter 1

Welcome to Larkhaven

At the Friday night market in Old Town, a singer opens her mouth and nothing comes out. Across the city, a nurse finishes her shift, a student skips a lecture, and a bus driver takes the last run of the night. By morning all three will be on the trail of the thing that stole that voice. They are witches, and this is their city.

What this game is

Witch & Ward is a tabletop roleplaying game for 3–5 players and a Storyteller. Each player is a witch in the coven, or one player may play the coven’s guardian. Together you protect innocent people from demons and other threats in Larkhaven, an invented harbour city.

It plays like an episode of a TV drama. Each session has a threat of the week, and an innocent: the person the threat wants. Around that runs witch life: jobs, family, friends, dates and rent. A witch can spend the night fighting a fire spirit and still have to explain to her boss why she’s late. The game is often funny and sometimes frightening.

One session is one episode. Each episode works on its own. You can also string episodes into a season, where a little carries over from one to the next (Chapter 8).

What you need

For one episode

  • 3–5 players and one Storyteller.
  • Six-sided dice. A shared pile of 13 covers every roll, even the biggest one: the Unmaking with five witches. In a season, once a witch has raised Craft to 4, that roll can need 14. A few dice each speeds things up.
  • A witch sheet for each witch, the guardian sheet if someone plays the guardian, and an episode tracker for the Storyteller. They are at the back of this book.
  • Pencils. Hurt, Magic and the tracks change all the time.
  • 2–3 hours.

For your first game, use the six ready-made witches and the ready-made guardian, Rook (Chapter 13). Chapter 12 walks the Storyteller through the first night.

Tone

This is a supernatural drama with humour. Witch life, family and romance run alongside the threat of the week. Play the funny moments for laughs and the scary ones straight.

The game is for ages 13 and up. Threats can be frightening, but there is no gore. Deaths happen off the page: the story can say that someone died, but it doesn’t show it. Nobody dies from the dice. A witch who is badly hurt goes Down, out of the fight, never dead. Romance is an optional story thread with no rules attached (Chapter 8).

Real faiths

Witchcraft and Wicca are living religions. Real people practise them today. The witches in this book are invented: their Powers, spells, potions and Book belong to this game, not to anyone’s real practice.

  • Don’t mock real practice.
  • Don’t use real symbols, such as the pentacle, as shorthand for evil. A threat is evil because of what it does, not because of a sign it wears.
  • The demons here are this game’s own creatures. No faith is the villain, and none is the joke.

Keeping the table safe

Before your first episode, take a few minutes to ask what anyone would rather not see in the game. Leave those things out. Nobody needs to say why.

Skip or fade

  • Anyone can say “skip” at any time. The story jumps past that moment.
  • Anyone can say “fade”. It still happens, but off the page, like a film cutting away.
  • No one has to explain. The Storyteller just moves on, and nobody argues.
  • At the end of the session, check in: was anything too much?

The coven

The coven is 2–5 witches, plus the guardian if a player plays it. Its members are family by blood or by choice: sisters, cousins, old friends, a lodger who stayed. They share a home, the Lantern House, and the coven’s spellbook, the Book.

Each witch has one Power (Force, Flame, Sight, Voice, Veil or Wild), a Life (a job or role outside magic) and four ratings. Chapter 3 shows you how to make one.

The coven is strongest together. The only spell that destroys a threat for good is the Unmaking, and every witch who joins it makes it stronger (Chapter 7).

The guardian

The guardian is a protective spirit, bound to the coven’s home by an old promise. It takes a human or an animal shape, and it answers to no master. It heals, shields, carries people to safety, senses what others miss and draws the threat’s attacks onto itself. It never casts spells and never destroys a threat. One player may play it instead of a witch (Chapter 4). If nobody does, the coven can still call on it once an episode.

The ready-made guardian is Rook. In human shape Rook is a very tall, very still person in a long grey coat. In animal shape, a large black rook.

The Storyteller

The Storyteller runs everything else: the city, its people, the threat and the innocent. They describe each scene, set how hard each roll is and roll for the threat. They aren’t playing against you. Their job is to make the threat frightening, give every witch a moment in the story, and follow the rules so the dice decide. Part Two of this book is for them.

Larkhaven

Larkhaven is a harbour city, set today. Fog in the mornings, ferries, steep streets, old money in the Heights and new flats on the old docks.

The Lantern House

The coven lives in a narrow, three-storey house in Old Town. A lamp over the door has never gone out. On the ground floor are the front room, the kitchen where potions get brewed, and a back door to a walled garden. The first floor has three bedrooms and a bathroom. The Book sits on a lectern in the attic, among old trunks, under a round window over the street. The guardian is bound to the house.

Threats can’t come inside uninvited. That is story, not a rule: no showdown ever happens here. Home is also where the coven can research the Book and brew potions without anyone seeing.

How an episode runs

The episode opens when the threat strikes. The coven then investigates in scenes. In each scene every character takes one action: following leads around the city, researching the Book, brewing, resting, covering up magic or answering the call of their everyday life. After every scene the threat’s Doom rises. With half the Clues the coven knows what the threat is and where it hides, and can go to it. With all of them it knows the threat’s Weakness. Wait too long, and the threat comes to the coven instead, in a public place, with the innocent in its grip. The showdown is fought in rounds until the threat is Broken and the coven joins together to unmake it, or it gets away, or the coven falls. A closing scene ties up lives and loose ends.

How to read this book

  • Part One (Chapters 1–8) is for everyone: the roll, witches, the guardian, magic, the episode, the showdown and seasons.
  • Part Two (Chapters 9–12) is for the Storyteller: running an episode, threats, making episodes and the first night.
  • Part Three (Chapters 13–16) is ready to play: the coven, the Lantern House and three episodes. Players shouldn’t read an episode before they play it.
  • The sheets are at the back.

Each rule is stated fully in one place. When a chapter needs a rule from another chapter, it says where to look. Game terms are in bold the first time a section uses them.
