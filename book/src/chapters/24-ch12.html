Chapter 12

The First Night

This chapter takes a new group from an empty table to the first scene in under 30 minutes. Nobody needs to make a character or learn the whole book. The players pick a ready-made witch, learn one roll, and play Episode 1: Night Market at Easy.

Before the night

The Storyteller reads Chapter 9 and Episode 1 (Chapter 14) before the group arrives. That takes about an hour, and it isn’t part of the 30 minutes.

Print these:

  • The six ready-made witches’ sheets, already filled in for Dana, Marco, June, Quinn, Tess and Noor (Chapter 13), so the players have a real choice.
  • Rook’s sheet, already filled in.
  • The episode tracker.
  • The quick reference. One for each player if you can, and one for you.

All four are at the back of the book. Bring pencils and erasers too.

Dice. You need only six-sided dice: at least 13 for the table to share. The biggest roll with the ready-made witches is the Unmaking with five witches: the leader’s Craft 3, plus 2 dice for each of the four who join, plus up to 2 extra dice, makes 13. Once ratings can reach 4 in a season, 14 dice cover the biggest roll: a five-witch Unmaking led by Craft 4 is 4 + 8 + 2. Dice are never compared, so one colour is fine. The Storyteller needs one die for the threat’s move table.

Keep the episode pages to yourself. Part Three is for the Storyteller. The players see their sheets, the tracker and the quick reference.

1. Welcome and tone

Tell the players what the game is: they are a coven of witches in the harbour city of Larkhaven, and tonight something is hunting an innocent person. Say the tone: for ages 13 and up, frightening but not gory, and deaths happen off the page. Ask if there is anything they don’t want in the game, and leave it out.

2. Pick witches

Lay out the six witch sheets. Each player picks one. If two players want the same witch, each rolls a die, and the highest picks; roll again on a tie. Give everyone a minute to read their Power and their Life. Every ready-made witch already has ratings, a Power, a Life, 4 Hurt boxes and 5 Magic.

3. Rook, or the call

One player may play Rook, the coven’s guardian, instead of a witch. Rook helps, heals and protects, but never casts spells, brews or damages a threat. Offer Rook to a player who likes looking after others. Only one player can play Rook: if two want to, each rolls a die, the highest plays Rook, and the other plays a witch.

If nobody plays Rook, Rook stays home guarding the house. Once per episode, on any character’s turn, the coven may call it for one Mend or one Carry. Calling takes no action and no roll, and counts as casting. Rook comes, does that one thing and goes home. Show the players the “guardian called” box on the tracker.

4. Fill in the tracker

Episode 1 is Easy. Use the Easy numbers from Chapter 6: Lead Difficulty 1; the threat’s Defence 1 (2 until the coven knows the Weakness), Danger 1 and Hit 2; Exposure 5 boxes; Doom 12 boxes with the mark at 5. Read Toughness, Clues needed, attacks a round and the Unmaking’s Difficulty off the lookup table in Chapter 9. Doom, Exposure and Clues start at 0.

Example: four players, one playing Rook

w = 3 witches and n = 4. At Easy: Toughness 3 × 3 = 9 (no extra 3, since a player plays the guardian). Clues needed 3 × 4 = 12, half 6. Exposure 5 boxes. Four characters, so the threat makes two attacks a round. The Unmaking’s Difficulty is 3, or 5 without the Weakness.

5. One practice roll

Teach the roll with one roll. Pick a player and have them roll their witch’s best rating against Difficulty 2. Read the result out together. The practice roll doesn’t count in the episode: it has no cost, and a Critical gives no extra die later.

Example: the practice roll

Dana climbs a locked gate: Nerve 3 against Difficulty 2. She rolls three dice: 6, 4, 2. Each 4, 5 or 6 is a success, so that’s two: a Success.

With one success it would be a Partial: half of 2, rounded up, is 1, so she gets over, with a cost. With none it would be a Failure: she doesn’t, and there’s a cost. Out of a fight, the cost is 1 Hurt, or Exposure +1 if she was casting out in the city. If two dice had shown 6, it would have been a Critical.

Her Power, Force, adds 2 dice when it fits. On top of that she can add up to 2 more: a friend’s help (1 Magic or Ward each, if they can help in the story), her Life when the Storyteller agrees it fits, the Book, or her own Magic (1 each).

At Easy, leads are Difficulty 1, so a lead has no Partial: one success finds a Clue.

6. How an episode runs

Explain this in five minutes, using the tracker:

  • Scenes. In the investigation, each character takes one action a scene: follow a lead, research the Book, brew, rest, cover up, or answer their Life. At the end of each scene, Doom +1.
  • Clues. With half the Clues, the coven knows what the threat is and where, and may go to it. With all of them, it knows the Weakness.
  • The mark. If Doom reaches 5, it’s too late: the threat comes to the coven in a public place, with the innocent in its grip.
  • Exposure. Casting where people can see raises it: a casting roll that costs something out in the city, and every round of casting in a public showdown. If it fills, the coven is Seen, and the result drops a step.
  • Magic. Unspent Magic is lost at the end of the episode, so spend it. But keep 2: 1 to join the Unmaking, and 1 for a die or a second try.

Leave the showdown rules until the showdown. Teach them when the fight starts.

7. The opening and the Life calls

Read the opening of Night Market. Then tell each witch what their Life wants from them this episode, from the episode’s Life-call table. They can answer it in any scene. Then start the first scene.

First-night checklist

  • Storyteller has read Chapter 9 and Episode 1.
  • Printed: six witch sheets filled in with the ready-made witches, Rook’s sheet, the episode tracker, quick references.
  • At least 13 six-sided dice, pencils and erasers.
  • Tone said; lines asked about.
  • Witches picked (same witch: roll, highest picks).
  • Rook played, or the “guardian called” box shown.
  • Tracker filled in at Easy; Doom, Exposure and Clues at 0.
  • One practice roll.
  • Scenes, Doom, the mark at 5, half and all the Clues, Exposure, and “keep 2 Magic” explained.
  • Life calls given out.
  • Episode pages kept away from the players.

After the first night

Easy is for learning. A coven almost never loses there. When the group knows the roll, move on.

  • Next, play Standard. Episode 2: Cinders is Standard. Witches start to go Down, and the threat sometimes reaches the mark.
  • Then Hard, when the group wants real danger. Episode 3: Low Tide is Hard.
  • Then roll your own with the generator in Chapter 11.

Seasons. Play the episodes in a row with the same coven and they become a season. Chapter 8 has the rules; in short:

  • A destroyed threat gets a page in the Book. Next time, the coven starts with half the Clues (rounded up).
  • A threat that wasn’t destroyed comes back when the Storyteller chooses, with 2 more Toughness for each time it escaped.
  • Hurt clears between episodes. The exception is a Scar: a witch who was Down when the coven lost (except by retreating) starts the next episode with 1 Hurt.
  • After every second episode, each witch raises one rating by 1 (most 4). Rook doesn’t grow.
  • After the 2nd and the 4th episodes, threats get 2 more Toughness per witch.

So if you play the three ready-made episodes as a season, Low Tide’s threat is tougher than the Hard column alone. Against 4 witches it has 4 × 4 + 2 × 4 = 24 Toughness. The witches are stronger too: each has raised a rating.
