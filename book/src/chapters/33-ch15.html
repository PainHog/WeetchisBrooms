Chapter 15

Episode 2: Cinders

Storyteller only

This chapter is for the Storyteller. Players shouldn’t read it before they play it: the fun is in finding out.

More than a hundred years ago, a bride died in a fire on her wedding night. Now the Ash Widow walks the Docks again, looking for the house where she died. She wants to burn it, with a bride inside, and a firefighter who is getting married next week has just bought it. This is a Standard episode.

The opening

Read this aloud, then ask the players where their witches were when they saw the smoke.

Saturday night on the Docks. Sirens. Smoke rolls up from Tanner’s Row, a terrace of old houses one street back from the water. From the hill you can see that this fire isn’t spreading the way fires do. It runs in a perfect circle through the back gardens, house to house, a ring of flame with one bright knot of fire at the top, like a wedding ring dropped on the city. The fire crews get everyone out. One firefighter stops in the street and stares into the flames, at a woman in a wedding dress burned grey-white, who stares back. The air smells of smoke and orange blossom. Then the woman is gone, and the ring goes out all at once.

Then tell each witch what their Life wants from them this episode (the Life calls table below).

The innocent: Hannah Price

Hannah is a firefighter at the Old Town fire station: practical, funny and fearless about everything except her own wedding. She marries Jess Moran next Saturday. They have just bought their first house, an old terrace at 7 Ropewalk Lane, one street over from Tanner’s Row. They move in after the wedding. Hannah was the firefighter in the street, and she hasn’t told anyone what she saw.

This week Larkhaven holds its harbour fireworks. Hannah’s crew is on standby on the quayside, and that is where the Ash Widow will take her if Doom reaches the Showdown mark.

The threat: the Ash Widow

Her stat block is in Chapter 10. She is a fire spirit in a burned wedding dress. In life she was Clara Wren. In 1911 she married Edwin Hale, and the wedding party was held at the Mariner Theatre on the Docks. That night their new house on Ropewalk Lane caught fire. Edwin died trying to reach her, and she died a few minutes later: a widow for those few minutes. Docks people have called her the Ash Widow ever since.

  • Plan: to burn the house where she died, with a bride inside.
  • Weakness: water from the harbour, blessed by a coven.
  • Hidden place: the burned-out theatre on the Docks (the Mariner).
  • Public place: the harbour fireworks.
  • Signature move (on a 6): Cinders: attack every character, but Hit is 1. If the innocent is there and free, she goes for her instead.

Go to her, or wait?

At the end of each scene, Doom +1. If Doom has reached 5, it’s too late: the Ash Widow comes to the coven at the harbour fireworks, with Hannah in her grip. If not, and the coven has half the Clues, it knows what she is and where she hides, and may go to the Mariner (this takes no scene). Hannah isn’t there.

Leads

Here are six leads to offer. Each says where it happens, the roll, and which Powers fit. Rolls are against the Lead Difficulty (2). A success gives 1 Clue, a Critical 2, a Partial 1 with a cost.

  • Use the What and where answer while the coven has fewer than half the Clues. Use the Weakness answer once it has half or more.
  • Players can come back to a lead. Show them something new there each time.
  • When the coven has all the Clues, tell them the whole Weakness plainly, whichever lead gave the last one.
  • Out in the city, a cost is Exposure +1 if the witch was casting (a Power or Reveal), or else 1 Hurt. Anyone else the coven talks to is a talking lead too.

1. Hannah Price (talking)

Where: the Old Town fire station, between shifts. Roll: Heart. Powers that fit: Sight, Voice, Wild.

What and where: In the flames at Tanner’s Row, a woman in a burned wedding dress asked her, in a voice like paper catching, “Are you to be married?” The ring of fire started at the end of the row nearest the old Mariner Theatre. Hannah mentions, without thinking, that she has just bought a house one street over.

Weakness: Water from the hoses, off the mains, did nothing. Only the fireboat’s pumps, drawing straight from the harbour, made the flames shrink back, “like it hurt”.

2. Winnie Doyle (talking)

Where: the Seaview care home on the Harbour. Winnie is 91 and ran the bar at the Mariner until it burned in 1979. Roll: Heart. Powers that fit: Sight, Voice, Wild.

What and where: Winnie tells the story of Clara Wren: married in 1911, the party at the Mariner, the fire on Ropewalk Lane that night. She saw the Ash Widow herself, standing on the Mariner’s stage the night it burned in 1979. “She sleeps in there. In the ashes.”

Weakness: Winnie’s mother told her that in 1911 the Old Town women put out the fire at the Mariner with buckets of harbour water, “and they said words over every bucket”. Then, quieter: “Clara Wren was one of them. That’s why they burned her house.”

3. Tanner’s Row (searching)

Where: the burned houses on the Docks, behind the fire service tape. Roll: Wits. Powers that fit: Sight, Veil, Wild.

What and where: Small bare footprints in the ash come from the direction of the Mariner Theatre and go round the row, door to door. Every door number is scorched away. She was looking for one house. In one letterbox is a charred wedding invitation: Hannah Price and Jess Moran, next Saturday.

Weakness: Wherever the fireboat’s harbour water pooled, the ash won’t settle. A scrap of burnt lace near a puddle curls away from it and crumbles.

4. The newspaper archive (searching)

Where: the university library on University Hill (the open floors, not the locked basement). A student’s Life might fit here. Roll: Wits. Powers that fit: Sight, Veil, Wild.

What and where: The Larkhaven Courier, 1911: “Bride dies in Ropewalk Lane fire on wedding night”, with a photograph of Clara Wren. The Mariner burned again in 1952 and 1979, both times on the night of a big wedding in the city. This year’s property notices: 7 Ropewalk Lane, sold to H. Price and J. Moran.

Weakness: A letter to the editor from 1911: the fire brigade couldn’t save the Mariner, but “a line of Old Town women with buckets from the harbour” put it out, “talking over every bucket like a church full of grandmothers”.

5. The Book (research at home)

Where: the attic of the Lantern House. Roll: Wits with +1 die (the Book), with no Exposure; one character per scene. Powers that fit: Sight.

What and where: A page on brides of ash: a woman who dies by fire on her wedding night may burn on until the house she died in burns again, with a bride inside. Between fires, she rests where she was last happy. For Clara, that was the stage of the Mariner.

Weakness: In the margin of that page, notes in a small neat hand, signed C. W.: harbour water, blessed by a coven, puts out any fire that walks. Clara Wren wrote in this Book. (This is the twist; see below.)

6. 7 Ropewalk Lane (Reveal)

Where: Hannah and Jess’s new house, empty and smelling of paint. Nothing ordinary shows here, so Heart and Wits find nothing. Roll: Reveal (Craft, 1 Magic); a success gives 1 more Clue. It is casting, so a cost is Exposure +1. Powers that fit: Sight.

What and where: Reveal shows the house as it was on the night in 1911: burning, with a woman in white at the upstairs window. A trail of glowing footprints leads out of the front door and down to the Mariner Theatre.

Weakness: At the end of the lane, on the old harbour steps, Reveal shows marks cut into the stone, glowing faintly. This is where Clara’s coven blessed the water.

The hidden place: the Mariner Theatre

A burned-out theatre in the middle of the Docks, between new flats and the container yard. Nobody has managed to knock it down. The coven goes to it with half the Clues; going takes no scene. Nothing here needs a roll to get past. If the coven retreats, everyone leaves together by any way out. Hannah isn’t here.

  • The front. Boarded doors under a sagging canopy, an old ticket booth and a sign: Danger. Keep Out.
  • The foyer. A black marble floor, a grand staircase that ends in empty air, and a wall of mirrors cracked by heat.
  • The stalls. Rows of burnt seats under a roof that is mostly sky. Ash drifts down like snow, though nothing is burning.
  • The stage. The boards are sound. A wedding arch of wire and wax flowers still stands at the back. This is where she sleeps.
  • The fly gallery. Iron walkways high above the stage, hung with old ropes.
  • Under the stage. Damp dressing rooms. At high tide, harbour water seeps in ankle-deep.

Ways in: the front doors (the boards come away); the stage door from the container yard; the loading dock at the side, with its roller door half up; the broken roof, from the warehouse next door. Ways out: the same four.

The Ash Widow rises out of the ash on the stage. The wax flowers on the arch melt, and every seat in the stalls catches light at once, though none of them burns away.

The public place: the harbour fireworks

If Doom reaches the Showdown mark, it is fireworks night, and half of Larkhaven is on the quayside. In a public place, every round in which anyone was casting raises Exposure by 1 (once per round). Nothing here needs a roll to get past.

  • The quayside. Thousands of people, food vans and music. Hannah’s fire engine is parked here, and the fireboat is moored at the quay.
  • The fireworks barge. Moored out in the harbour. The show is fired from here.
  • The ferry terminal. A glass hall, open late for the festival.
  • The fish market roof. A flat roof where people climb for a better view.
  • The promenade. The wide walk from Old Town to the long pier.
  • The harbour steps. Stone steps down to the water, where small boats tie up.

Ways in and out: the promenade from Old Town; through the ferry terminal; the fish market lane; by boat to the harbour steps.

In her grip: as the first rocket goes up, it bursts white and falls burning onto the quay, and the Ash Widow walks out of the sparks with her arms round Hannah. Hannah’s helmet is melting. To free her, roll Nerve against Defence; only a success frees her. If she is still in her grip at the end of the first round, she is lost. Once she is free, Rook (or the coven’s call) can Carry her to a place it knows, such as the Lantern House.

The twist

Clara Wren was a witch, and she lived in the Lantern House before she married. Her notes are in the margins of the Book. In 1911, someone saw her cast and talked. By her wedding night, half the Docks believed she was a witch, and somebody set her new house alight. Her coven blessed harbour water and saved the Mariner, but they were too late for her.

The twist comes out on Winnie’s lead and in the Book, or in the showdown, when the Ash Widow looks at the coven and whispers, “You’re from the lamp house. So was I.” It is a hard reminder of why magic stays secret. The twist changes no rules.

Endings

Win (she is destroyed, and Hannah is safe). The blessed water hisses over her. The fire goes out of her dress, and for a moment she is a young woman in white, smiling. “Tell Edwin I waited,” she says, and she is gone. On Saturday, Hannah marries Jess.

Partial (she fled, or she was destroyed but Hannah was lost). If she fled, she sinks into the ashes of the Mariner and waits for the next wedding in Larkhaven. Hannah marries, but every wedding in the city this year has a fire engine parked outside. If she was destroyed but Hannah was lost, the Widow took Hannah into the fire with her as she went out, and no one finds her. Jess keeps the keys to Ropewalk Lane and won’t sell.

Loss (Doom filled, every witch Down, or the coven retreated). If Doom filled, her plan is complete: Hannah is lost and the Ash Widow escapes. On the night before the wedding, 7 Ropewalk Lane burns again, in a perfect ring, with Hannah inside. If every witch went Down or the coven retreated, she walks away through the flames, still asking everyone she passes if they are to be married.

Seen (the result drops one step; a Loss stays a Loss). The fireworks crowd filmed everything. By morning, “Larkhaven’s burning bride” and the people who fought her are on every phone in the city. Or, if the coven was Seen during the investigation, someone caught a witch casting at Tanner’s Row, and the fire service wants a word.

Carry-over. If she was destroyed, the Ash Widow gets a page in the Book (next time, start with half the Clues). If she wasn’t, she comes back later with 2 more Toughness. A witch who is Down when the coven loses gets a Scar: 1 Hurt at the start of the next episode.

Closing scene

Saturday comes. Give each player a short Life scene: what they missed this week, and who forgave them. Then go to the wedding, if there is one, or to the empty house on Ropewalk Lane if there isn’t. Hannah raises a glass “to absent friends” and looks straight at the coven. End in the attic of the Lantern House, where the coven opens the Book to Clara Wren’s neat notes and decides what to write beside them.
