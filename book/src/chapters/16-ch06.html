Chapter 6

The Episode

An episode is one evening’s story. The threat strikes. The coven pieces together what it is and where it hides, while the threat’s plan creeps forward. In the end, someone faces it.

The shape of an episode

  1. Opening. The threat strikes.
  2. Investigation, in scenes. The coven looks for Clues.
  3. Showdown, in rounds. The coven fights the threat (Chapter 7).
  4. Closing. Life scenes, consequences, carry-over.

The episode’s numbers

Each episode has a label: Easy, Standard or Hard. At the start, the Storyteller sets every number from this table. The numbers don’t change if someone joins or leaves during the episode. In the table, w is the number of witches and n is the number of witches plus the guardian, if a player plays it.

Doom always has 12 boxes, with the Showdown mark at 5. A coven of 3 witches with no guardian player faces 3 more Toughness, and a season adds more (Chapters 7 and 8).

Opening

The episode starts when the threat strikes: a singer loses her voice, a row of houses burns, three people walk into the sea. Doom, Exposure and Clues all start at 0. If the Book has a page on this kind of threat, the coven starts with half the Clues (rounded up) instead (Chapter 5).

Scenes

The investigation is played in scenes. In each scene, every character takes one action, in any order. At the end of the scene, Doom rises by 1.

Leads

A lead is any action that can find Clues. A talking lead uses Heart: questioning a witness, comforting someone who’s scared, lying your way past a security desk. A searching lead uses Wits: going over a scene, reading old records, following tracks. Researching the Book is a lead using Wits at home. Reveal is a lead using Craft.

Lead results

Critical: 2 Clues. Success: 1 Clue. Partial: 1 Clue, with a cost. Failure: no Clue, and a cost.

Reveal gives 1 more Clue on a success. Sight, Voice and Wild can Surge for 1 more Clue on a lead they fit.

Out in the city, a casting roll’s cost is Exposure +1; any other cost is 1 Hurt (Chapter 2). The Storyteller tells you what each Clue shows: a name, a smell of smoke, a pattern on a map.

Clues

  • Half the Clues (rounded up): the coven knows what the threat is and where it is, and may go to it.
  • All the Clues: the coven knows the threat’s Weakness. Until it does, the threat’s Defence is 1 higher and the Unmaking is 2 harder (Chapter 7).
  • Clues past the total are lost.

Clues found in the showdown, by studying the threat, count too.

Doom

Doom is the threat’s plan. It has 12 boxes, and it rises by 1 at the end of every scene and every round. Nothing lowers it.

  • The Showdown mark (5): if Doom has reached it at the end of a scene, the threat comes to the coven. The mark is checked only then, even if Doom rose during the scene.
  • Full (12): the plan is complete. The innocent is lost, the threat escapes, and the episode ends as a Loss.
  • Doom doesn’t rise once the threat is Broken.

The end of a scene

1. Doom +1.

2. If Doom has reached the Showdown mark (5), it’s too late. The threat comes to the coven. The showdown starts in a public place, with the innocent in its grip, whatever the coven did for them before.

3. Otherwise, if the coven has half the Clues, it may go to the threat now. Going takes no scene. The showdown is in a hidden place, and the innocent isn’t there. Or the coven plays another scene.

Go now, or one more scene?

If nothing else raises Doom, it reaches 4 at the end of the fourth scene. That is the last moment to go to the threat. Play a fifth scene and Doom reaches 5: the threat comes to you. So if you have half the Clues but not all of them, you face a real choice.

Neither is always right. Count how many Clues you are short. Studying the threat can find the last few in the fight. Ask whether your coven can free the innocent in round 1, and how close Exposure is to full. You can also go earlier, at the end of any scene, once you have half the Clues.

Exposure and Seen

Exposure is how close ordinary people are to seeing magic. Its number of boxes depends on the label.

  • It rises by 1 when a casting roll out in the city has a cost, and by 1 for each round of a public showdown in which anyone was casting (Chapter 7).
  • It falls when someone casts Hide (Chapter 5), never below 0.

Seen

When Exposure is full, the coven is Seen. Exposure stays full, and Hide can’t lower it. Any further rise becomes Doom +1 instead (once per round or scene), but not once the threat is Broken. At the end, Seen drops the episode’s result one step.

Being Seen is costly. A Win becomes a Partial and a Partial becomes a Loss. Every careless spell after that pushes the threat’s plan closer. In the story, there are phone videos, a reporter asking questions, neighbours who stop waving.

Your Life call

Each witch’s Life calls once per episode. Answering it is an action in any scene: no roll, and you gain 1 Magic. If you haven’t answered by the showdown, you lose 1 Magic, or take 1 Hurt if you have none (Chapter 3). The Storyteller frames the call; the player decides when to answer.

The closing

After the showdown comes the closing: the result (Chapter 7), Life scenes and consequences. This is where the everyday side of the game gets its moment: the date that got cut short, the shift a friend covered, the innocent saying thank you, or not. In a season, note what carries over (Chapter 8).

Example: a scene in Cinders

A Standard episode: the Ash Widow is burning houses, and Hannah Price, a firefighter about to be married, is the innocent. The coven is Dana (Force), Marco (Flame) and June (Sight), and the fourth player plays Rook. That is 4 characters, so the coven needs 4 × 4 − 1 = 15 Clues, and half is 8. The Lead Difficulty is 2.

At the start of scene 3, Doom is 2, Exposure is 1 of 4 and the coven has 6 Clues. June has already answered her Life. Dana, Marco and June have 5 Magic each, and Rook has 5 Ward.

June searches the burned row of houses: a searching lead, Wits 3 plus 2 for Sight. Rook uses Sense (4 Ward left) for 1 more die, outside the limit. June rolls 6 dice: 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1. Three successes, but only one 6: a Success, 1 Clue. Clues: 7.

Dana talks to a nurse at Larkhaven General who treated the people from the fire. Dana is a nurse, so the Storyteller agrees her Life fits: Heart 2 plus 1. She rolls 4, 2, 1: one success, a Partial. 1 Clue (Clues: 8), and since she wasn’t casting, the cost is 1 Hurt.

Marco answers his Life: a customer’s van has to be ready tonight. No roll; he gains 1 Magic, to 6.

Rook talks to an old woman who saw the first fire: Heart 3. Rook rolls 5, 4, 1: a Success, 1 Clue. Clues: 9.

End of scene 3. Doom rises to 3: not the mark. The coven has 9 Clues, more than half, so it knows what the threat is (a fire spirit in a burned wedding dress) and where (the burned-out theatre on the Docks). It could go now, but it is 6 Clues short of the Weakness. It plays another scene.

Scene 4. Dana answers her Life, a double shift, and gains 1 Magic, to 6. June casts Reveal from across the street from the theatre: Craft 2 plus 2 for Sight, 1 Magic (4 left). She rolls 6, 4, 4, 1: a Success, 1 Clue plus 1 for Reveal. Marco talks to the fire investigator whose truck he fixes; his Life fits, so he rolls Heart 2 plus 1: 5, 5, 2, a Success. Rook searches the fire scene with Wits 2: 6, 4, a Success. Clues: 9 + 2 + 1 + 1 = 13. Doom rises to 4.

The choice. Thirteen of 15. One more scene would probably find the Weakness, but Doom would reach 5, and the Ash Widow would come to them at the harbour fireworks with Hannah in her grip. The coven goes now. In the fight, June (Wits 3 plus Sight) and Rook can study the threat for the last 2 Clues. Chapter 7 picks up the fight.
