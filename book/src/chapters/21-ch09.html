Chapter 9

Running an Episode

You are the Storyteller. You play Larkhaven and everyone in it, the threat included. The players have the witches and the guardian. Your job is to give them a frightening night, real choices and a fair fight.

The Storyteller’s job

  • Set the scene: where the coven is, what they see, who is there.
  • Set the Difficulty when the label doesn’t.
  • Say the cost of a Partial or a Failure.
  • Run the threat by its d6 table. You roll; the table decides.
  • Keep the tracks on the episode tracker.

You are not playing against the players. The dice bring the danger; you bring the city to life. Be a fan of the witches: make their wins feel big and their losses feel real.

When the rules don’t set a Difficulty, choose 1 to 4. With 3 dice, a witch succeeds about 88% of the time at Difficulty 1, 50% at 2 and 13% at 3. At 4 they need extra dice.

Choosing the label

Every episode has a label: Easy, Standard or Hard. It sets the episode’s numbers from the table in Chapter 6: the Lead Difficulty, the threat’s stats, the Clues needed and the Exposure boxes. Set every number at the start of the episode; they don’t change if someone joins or leaves. Below, w is the number of witches and n is the witches plus the guardian, if a player plays it.

Here is what each label feels like, from 36,000 simulated episodes.

  • Easy is for first nights and new players. About 85% of Easy episodes end in a Win and the rest in a Partial; almost none end in a Loss. A witch goes Down in only 2% of episodes, and the threat almost never reaches the Showdown mark.
  • Standard is the normal game. About 73% Wins and 8% Losses. A witch goes Down in about two episodes in five (39%), and about one in five (21%) ends with the threat coming to the coven in a public place.
  • Hard is for a coven that wants real danger. About 59% Wins and 23% Losses. A witch goes Down in most episodes (61%), and about a third (34%) end in a public showdown.

Group size moves these by a few points. At Standard, for example, Wins run from 67% (five witches) to 79% (two witches and a guardian player). Start new players on Easy.

Setting up the episode tracker

  1. Count the coven: w and n.
  2. Clues needed: mark the total, and half, rounded up.
  3. Toughness: cross out the boxes you don’t need. Against 3 witches with no guardian player, the threat has 3 more.
  4. Exposure: 5 boxes at Easy, 4 at Standard and Hard. Doom: 12 boxes, the mark at 5.
  5. The threat’s line: Defence (1 higher until the coven knows the Weakness), Danger, Hit, attacks a round (1 against 3 characters, 2 against 4 or 5), and the Unmaking’s Difficulty (the number of witches, +2 without the Weakness).
  6. No guardian player? Leave the “guardian called” box empty.

Doom, Exposure and Clues start at 0. Put the tracker where everyone can see Doom.

Framing scenes and leads

The opening. The threat strikes: something strange and frightening happens to someone in Larkhaven. Then tell each witch what their Life wants this episode. They can answer it in any scene, so they need to know from the start.

A scene. The investigation runs in scenes. In each one:

  1. Say what has changed and where things stand. Offer two or three leads.
  2. Go round the table. Each character takes one action: follow a lead out in the city, research the Book at home, brew at home, rest, cover up with Hide, or answer their Life. The guardian can research the Book but can’t brew.
  3. Resolve each roll and mark its cost.
  4. Doom +1. Show the threat’s plan moving: a new victim, a new sign.
  5. Check the Showdown mark. If Doom has reached 5, the threat comes to the coven. If not, and the coven has half the Clues, it may go to the threat.

Before the showdown the threat has no attacks. It shows itself through Doom and through costs.

Leads. A talking lead uses Heart: a witness, a neighbour, a nurse on the night shift. A searching lead uses Wits: a burned room, an old map, a phone full of photos. Reveal is a lead using Craft (1 Magic). All three happen out in the city, against the Lead Difficulty. The Book is a lead at home: Wits, +1 die, no Exposure, one character per scene. The written leads are a starting kit, not a fence: when a player thinks of someone to ask or somewhere to look, that’s a lead too. A lead can be followed again; show something new there each time.

What Clues tell. The first few show what is happening. At half, tell the coven plainly what the threat is and name its hidden place. With all of them, tell them its Weakness. Extra Clues past the total are lost.

Example: go now, or stay?

Standard. Dana, June and Quinn are the witches, and a fourth player plays Rook, so w = 3 and n = 4. The coven needs 15 Clues; half is 8. At the end of scene 4, Doom rises from 3 to 4. The coven has 9 Clues, so it knows the threat is the Ash Widow, hiding in the burned-out theatre on the Docks.

If they go now, the fight is in the theatre and Hannah, the innocent, isn’t there. But without the Weakness the Ash Widow’s Defence is 3, not 2, and the Unmaking needs 5 successes, not 3. If they stay to hunt the last 6 Clues, Doom reaches 5 at the end of the next scene, and the Ash Widow comes to them at the harbour fireworks with Hannah in her grip. They go.

When a Life fits

A witch’s Life adds 1 die, inside the +2 limit, when you agree it fits. Say yes when the job gives a real edge: the nurse knows how a hospital works at night, the bus driver knows every street and every regular, the student can get into the library after hours. About one roll in three is a good rate.

The Life also calls once per episode. Answering takes the witch’s action for that scene, needs no roll, and gives 1 Magic. Play it as a short scene: a shift, a lesson, a family dinner. If a witch hasn’t answered by the showdown, they lose 1 Magic (or take 1 Hurt if they have none). Make the call matter.

Costs, and what to describe

A Partial works, with a cost. A Failure doesn’t work, and there is a cost. The rules set the cost:

  • In a showdown: 1 Hurt.
  • Otherwise: Exposure +1 if the witch was casting out in the city, or else 1 Hurt.

Casting means using a Power, a spell, a potion or a guardian gift. Hide’s cost is always 1 Hurt. Book research and brewing are at home, so their cost is 1 Hurt.

Mark the cost first, then describe it. A Hurt is a twisted ankle, a cut hand, a bad scare. An Exposure is a passer-by filming, a scream, a story in the morning paper. Never add a second cost. Before a risky roll, say what it could cost.

A Critical does one thing extra, as fits: +1 damage, +1 Clue, +1 potion or −1 more Exposure. If none fits, the witch gets +1 die on their next roll this episode (inside the +2 limit). You decide which fits.

Running the threat

In the showdown the threat acts once a round: first in round 1, then after the characters in every later round. On its turn:

  1. Count its attacks: 1 against 3 characters, 2 against 4 or 5. Like every number, this is set at the start of the episode.
  2. Lost attacks. If Bind, Force’s Surge or a Binding potion made it lose its next attack, cross one off. It loses at most one attack a round; any more are wasted.
  3. For each attack left, roll a d6 and read the move table in Chapter 10, which also says whom each signature move targets. Roll even for an attack the guardian’s Hold has aimed. On a 6 the signature move still happens (or a free, unguarded innocent is taken); if the move picks targets, the guardian is the first, and Hold is used up.
  4. Before the target rolls, the guardian may Shield (1 Ward, once a round: it takes the attack instead). Then a Sight witch may Surge (2 Magic, once per showdown) to make it miss. Shield is chosen first. Someone under Shelter can’t be hurt by its attacks and doesn’t roll to defend.
  5. The target rolls Nerve, plus a Power that fits (Sight or Veil), against Danger. Success: no Hurt. Partial: 1 Hurt. Failure: Hurt equal to Hit.

The table decides who it attacks; you don’t hold back or go easy. Your only choices are ties, where the table or a signature move says you pick, and how the attack looks. Roll the d6 in the open. When the threat is Broken, it makes no more rolls or moves.

Example: the threat’s turn

Round 2 of a public showdown at Standard. The Ash Widow has Danger 3 and Hit 2. There are four characters, so she makes two attacks. Dana freed Hannah in round 1. This round Dana hit the Ash Widow with Force, so Dana hurt her last, and Rook is guarding Hannah.

First attack: the d6 shows 3, so she goes for whoever hurt her last: Dana. Force doesn’t fit defending, so Dana rolls Nerve 3: 5, 4, 1. Two successes against Danger 3 is a Partial: 1 Hurt.

Second attack: the d6 shows 6. Hannah is there and free, so instead of her signature move the Ash Widow goes for Hannah. Rook is guarding Hannah, so the Ash Widow attacks Rook. Rook rolls Nerve 2 and spends 1 Ward for a third die: 6, 4, 2. Two successes: a Partial, 1 Hurt.

Dana used Force this round, which is casting in a public place, so Exposure +1. Then Doom +1.

The innocent

The innocent is the person the threat wants. Give them a name, a want and a reason to be liked. During the investigation the coven can talk to them, warn them and keep them close.

But if Doom reaches the Showdown mark, the threat comes to the coven in a public place with the innocent in its grip, whatever the coven did for them before. Say that plainly when the players plan.

  • To free them, a character rolls Nerve against Defence. Only a success frees them; a Partial doesn’t, and still costs 1 Hurt. Force fits.
  • If they are still in its grip at the end of the first round, they are lost.
  • While in its grip they can’t be guarded or carried. Once free, a character can guard them, and the guardian (or the coven’s call) can Carry them away.
  • Once free, if the threat takes them, they are lost. A Broken threat lets go.

In a hidden showdown the innocent isn’t there. If Doom fills, the plan is complete and the innocent is lost. If the coven retreats, or every witch is Down, an innocent still in the fight is lost; one already carried to safety isn’t.

Other people the coven meets don’t need stats. Talking to them is a lead against the Lead Difficulty.

Public showdowns and Exposure

Exposure is how close ordinary people are to seeing magic. Out in the city it rises when a casting roll (other than Hide) costs something. In a public showdown it rises by 1 in every round in which anyone was casting (once per round). That makes every Power die a choice: a witch may leave their Power off a roll, and then it isn’t casting. The guardian’s Hold isn’t casting either. Veil’s Surge, used with Hide, hides casting for this round and the next. Hide (Difficulty 2) lowers Exposure by 1, never below 0, until the coven is Seen. A hidden showdown has no such rise. Describe the crowd: people film, shout, run and stare.

Seen

When Exposure is full, the coven is Seen. It stays full, and Hide can no longer lower it. Any further rise becomes Doom +1 instead (once per round or scene), except once the threat is Broken. At the end, being Seen drops the result one step (Chapter 7). Show it in the closing: a video that went round, a reporter at the door.

Keeping the round order

Round 1 runs: the threat’s attacks, the characters’ actions, the innocent, Exposure, Doom. Later rounds run: the characters’ actions, the threat’s attacks, Exposure, Doom. The characters act in any order; each takes one action or does nothing. The episode ends at once, and the round’s later steps don’t happen, when the threat is destroyed, flees, or Doom fills, or when the coven retreats. It also ends when every witch is Down at the end of a round. Keep the box below in front of you.

The Storyteller’s round

  1. Retreat? At the start of any round, the coven may retreat. Everyone leaves together. It’s a Loss, but nobody gets a Scar. An innocent still in the fight is lost.
  2. Attacks and actions. Round 1: the threat attacks first, then the characters act. Later rounds: the characters act, then the threat attacks. For each attack, roll a d6 on the move table; the target rolls Nerve against Danger.
  3. The innocent (round 1 only). Still in its grip? They are lost.
  4. Exposure. In a public place, if anyone was casting this round: +1, once. If it was already full: Doom +1 instead (not once the threat is Broken).
  5. Doom +1 (not once the threat is Broken). Every witch Down? It’s a Loss, and an innocent still in the fight is lost. Broke last round? It flees.

Endings

Chapter 7 has the results: Win, Partial and Loss, what Seen does to them, and Scars. Say the result plainly, then play the closing: Life scenes and consequences, such as a date that went well or a shift that went badly. Nobody dies from the dice. Chapter 8 has what carries over: Book pages, returning threats, Scars and growth.

Pacing a session

An episode takes 2 to 3 hours. Doom is your clock: one box per scene, one per round.

Tone

  • 13 and up. Supernatural drama with humour. Witch life, family and romance run alongside the threat.
  • Frightening, not gory. Show the fear: the cold, the silence, a voice that’s wrong. Don’t show wounds in detail.
  • Deaths happen off the page. A lost innocent is gone: taken, never found, or a name in the paper. You never describe a death.
  • Romance is an optional story thread with no rules, mostly with people from the witches’ everyday lives.
  • Respect real faiths. Real witchcraft and Wicca are living religions. Don’t mock them, and don’t use real symbols, such as the pentacle, as a sign of evil. The demons and spirits here belong to this game, not to any real religion.
  • Ask first. Before the first night, ask the players if there’s anything they don’t want in the game, and leave it out.
