Chapter 5

Magic

Magic in Larkhaven is real, and it is hard to hide. Every witch can cast the spells in the Book, brew potions in the kitchen and use their own Power. All of it costs something, and some of it can be seen.

Casting

Casting means using a Power, a spell, a potion or a guardian gift. Helping someone, adding your own Magic die, your Life die or the Book die doesn’t count as casting. A helper’s die or the guardian’s Sense doesn’t make your roll casting either, and the guardian’s Hold isn’t casting.

Where you cast decides what it risks:

WhereCost of a casting rollExposure
At home (the Lantern House)1 HurtNever rises
Out in the city (investigation)Exposure +1Rises only from that cost
Showdown, hidden place1 HurtNever rises
Showdown, public place1 Hurt+1 for each round in which anyone was casting

Casting Hide never raises Exposure, so its cost is always 1 Hurt. If you leave your Power off, the roll isn’t casting, unless it is a spell or uses a potion (Chapter 2). Exposure and being Seen are in Chapter 6.

Casting with no roll, such as Mend, Carry or using a potion, never raises Exposure out in the city. In a public showdown it counts toward that round’s casting like any other (Chapter 7).

Spells

Any witch can cast any spell. A spell is a Craft roll and costs 1 Magic. A Partial works, with a cost. A Failure doesn’t work, and there is a cost.

Reveal

Reveal is a lead, so it follows the lead results (Chapter 6), plus 1 Clue on a Success. That makes a Success worth 2 Clues, a Critical 3, and a Partial 1 Clue with a cost. Out in the city, Reveal is always casting, so its cost is Exposure.

Bind

The threat loses its next attack. A threat loses at most one attack a round, whatever the cause. If a second Bind, a Force Surge or a Binding potion lands on the same round of attacks, it is wasted. In round 1 the threat attacks before anyone acts, so a Bind cast in round 1 takes away one of its attacks in round 2. Wild’s Surge goes with Bind: the threat’s Defence is 1 lower for the rest of the round, starting after the Bind roll (Chapter 3).

Blast

Blast is an attack that uses Craft instead of Nerve (Chapter 7). A Success does 3 damage, a Critical 4 and a Partial 2 with a cost. Once the coven knows the Weakness, add 1 more.

Shelter

Shelter stops the threat’s attacks from hurting one person. It doesn’t stop the cost of that person’s own rolls. It can’t stop the threat taking a freed innocent either, because taking isn’t an attack (Chapter 7). Nobody can end a Shelter early. If the cost of your own Shelter roll puts you Down, it ends at once. A guardian under Shelter can’t Shield or Hold, and any Hold it has waiting ends (Chapter 4).

Hide

Hide calms witnesses and covers up what they saw. A Critical lowers Exposure 1 more. In a showdown, Hide is the spell to take Veil’s Surge: a defence roll isn’t an action, so it can’t carry a Surge. Veil’s Surge hides all the coven’s casting, Hide’s own roll included (Chapter 3). Exposure never goes below 0, and once the coven is Seen, Hide can’t lower it.

Any other spell

You can describe any spell you like. The Storyteller uses the closest spell on the list. If your idea stretches it, the Difficulty is 1 higher. Making the harbour fog roll in so nobody sees you leave is Hide. Making the reeds wrap round the creature’s legs is Bind. A ring of salt round Dana is Shelter. Asking an old photograph what it saw is Reveal, at +1 Difficulty, because it’s a stretch.

Rhymes are welcome. Say one aloud if you enjoy it, and make it your own. A rhyme gives no bonus.

Potions

Brewing

You brew at home, in the Lantern House kitchen. Brewing takes your action for the scene, or it can be the extra roll the Book researcher makes (below). Roll Craft against the Lead Difficulty.

Wild fits brewing, and Wild’s Surge makes 1 more potion. Choose the kind of each potion as you make it.

Using potions

Using a potion takes no action. Use it while the characters act, before or after your own action, on yourself or someone near you (the guardian included: it can’t use potions, but you can use one on it). In a showdown, that is never on the threat’s turn. Each character uses at most one potion a round in a showdown, or one a scene in the investigation. The limit counts whoever uses the potion, not whoever it is used on. Using a potion is casting.

A Blasting or Binding potion goes with the attacker’s own attack. An attack takes at most one potion.

A Down character can’t use potions at all, and Static stops a character using one with their next action (Chapter 10). Anyone else can still use a potion on them. A Healing potion brings a Down character back up.

Say you are using a potion before you roll. It is used up either way.

The coven shares its potions and can have at most one per character. The guardian counts if a player plays it. Extra potions from a brew are lost.

Potions spoil at the end of the episode.

The Weakness adds 1 damage to an attack that works, but not to a potion. A working attack with a Blasting potion, once the coven knows the Weakness, does its normal damage +1 for the Weakness +2 for the potion.

The Book

The Book is the coven’s spellbook. It lives on a lectern in the attic of the Lantern House, full of other witches’ notes, drawings and warnings.

Researching the Book

Researching the Book is an action at home. It is a searching lead using Wits, with +1 die (inside the +2 limit), and it never raises Exposure. Only one character can research the Book each scene. That character may also make one brewing roll in the same scene. Each roll has its own result and its own cost.

Pages. When the coven destroys a threat, the threat gets a page in the Book. The next time the coven faces that kind of threat, it starts the episode with half the Clues, rounded up. Pages last for the whole season (Chapter 8).

What magic can’t do

No spell can

  • Heal. Hurt clears only by resting, a Healing potion or the guardian’s Mend.
  • Bring back the dead.
  • Change the past.
  • Force love.

No stretch makes these possible. If a player describes one, the Storyteller says no and helps them find a spell that can work.

Example: covering up

In a Standard episode, the coven’s Exposure is at 3 of its 4 boxes after Marco set a skip on fire in front of a bus queue. Quinn Abernathy goes back to cover it up: Hide, out in the city. Quinn rolls Craft 2, plus 2 for Voice, which fits Hide: 4 dice against Difficulty 2. Quinn pays 1 Magic for the spell and 2 for a Surge before rolling: 3 Magic.

Quinn rolls 6, 4, 2, 1: two successes, a Success. Hide lowers Exposure by 1 and the Surge by 1 more, from 3 to 1. By the time Quinn leaves, the bus queue remembers a dropped cigarette.

Example: brewing

The coven is Dana, June, Noor and Rook, played by the fourth player, so it can hold at most 4 potions. It already has 2. In a Standard episode, Noor Haddad brews as her action: Craft 3, plus 2 for Wild, which fits brewing. She rolls 5 dice against the Lead Difficulty of 2: 6, 6, 4, 2, 1. Three successes and two 6s: a Critical, three potions.

Only 2 more fit, so Noor picks a Blasting potion and a Healing potion. The third potion is lost. She was casting at home, so even a short roll would have cost only Hurt, never Exposure.
