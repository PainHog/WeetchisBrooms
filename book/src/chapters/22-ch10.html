Chapter 10

Threats

Every episode has one threat: a demon, a spirit, a rogue witch, a cursed thing or a creature. It wants something, and Doom shows how close it is to getting it. This chapter shows how threats work, gives the one table every threat uses in a fight, and describes the six threats that haunt Larkhaven.

How threat stats work

Every threat has four stats. Defence is the Difficulty to hurt it. Danger is the Difficulty to defend against it. Hit is the Hurt it does when a defence fails. Toughness is the damage that Breaks it.

A threat’s numbers come from its episode’s label, not from the threat: its Defence, Danger, Hit and Toughness are in the numbers table in Chapter 6. Each stat block below repeats them for convenience. The Ash Widow is as tough as the Quiet Man at the same label. What makes them different is the story: the plan, the Weakness, the places and the signature move.

Toughness depends on w, the number of witches. The guardian doesn’t count. Against a coven of 3 witches and no guardian player, the threat has 3 more Toughness. Damage past its Toughness is lost.

Example: Toughness

The Mirror Thief at Hard, against June, Tess and Noor with no guardian player: 4 × 3 + 3 = 15 Toughness. If a fourth player joins as Rook before the episode starts, it’s 4 × 3 = 12, since the extra 3 is only against 3 witches with no guardian player.

The Weakness. Until the coven knows it, the threat’s Defence is 1 higher. Once it knows it, attack rolls (including Blast) that work do 1 more damage; potions don’t. Without the Weakness, the Unmaking is 2 harder.

Attacks. A threat makes 1 attack a round against 3 characters, and 2 against 4 or 5. It loses at most one attack a round, whatever the cause; any more are wasted.

Broken. When its Toughness is full, it is Broken. It makes no more rolls or moves, Doom stops rising, and it flees at the end of the next round. Only the Unmaking destroys it. If it gets away, it has been driven off.

Seasons. Threats grow during a season. Chapter 8 has the rules; in short:

  • After the 2nd and the 4th episodes, threats get 2 more Toughness per witch (so 4 more per witch from the 5th episode on).
  • A threat that wasn’t destroyed comes back when the Storyteller chooses, with 2 more Toughness for each time it escaped.
  • A destroyed threat gets a page in the Book. Next time, the coven starts with half the Clues (rounded up).

For example, in the 3rd episode of a season, a Standard threat against 4 witches has 3 × 4 + 2 × 4 = 20 Toughness. From the 5th episode on, it has 3 × 4 + 4 × 4 = 28.

The move table

All threats share one move table: roll a d6 for each attack (Chapter 7 has it in full). On 1–3 it attacks whoever hurt it last, on 4–5 whoever has the most Hurt, and on a 6 it uses its signature move, unless the innocent is there and free. What follows is what the Storyteller needs to run it.

  • Defending. The target rolls Nerve, plus a Power that fits (Sight or Veil), against Danger. Success: no Hurt. Partial: 1 Hurt. Failure: Hurt equal to Hit.
  • The innocent. Taking them isn’t an attack, so Hold can’t stop it. A freed innocent who is taken is lost.
  • Signature move targets. A move picks only standing characters. A move’s “one attack” is aimed like a 4–5: whoever has the most Hurt marked and is still standing. “Two different characters” means the most Hurt and the next most Hurt, both standing; if only one character is standing, it is one attack. A move that attacks several characters makes one attack on each, and each defends on their own. Ties: roll a die among them (Chapter 7).
  • Hold. If the guardian’s Hold works, the threat’s next attack must target the guardian. Still roll the d6. On a 6 the signature move still happens (or a free, unguarded innocent is taken); if it is an attacking move, the guardian is its first target, and Hold is used up. An attack on the innocent’s guard goes to the guardian instead (Hold is used up); taking an unguarded innocent isn’t an attack, so Hold stays. Hold aims only the attack in a move: under Hold, Lure’s attack goes to the guardian, and its Heart roll still happens. Hold isn’t casting.
  • Shield, Shelter and Sight. The guardian’s Shield takes an attack meant for someone near it; in a showdown, everyone is near. On the same attack, Shield is chosen before Sight’s Surge. Someone under Shelter can’t be hurt by the threat’s attacks and doesn’t roll to defend. Sight’s Surge makes one attack miss; that isn’t a lost attack. Against a move that attacks several characters, Shield, Shelter and Sight’s miss each cover only one of those attacks, and a guardian who Shields defends once for each attack it takes.

Example: a signature move

A hidden showdown at Standard: the Ash Widow (Danger 3) against Marco, Tess and Noor. The innocent isn’t there, so a 6 means her signature move, Cinders: one attack on each standing character, at Hit 1. All three are standing, so she makes three attacks, and each of them defends.

Marco rolls Nerve 3: 6, 4, 2. Two successes: a Partial, 1 Hurt. Tess rolls Nerve 3 plus Veil’s 2 dice: 5, 5, 4, 3, 1. Three successes: no Hurt. Noor rolls Nerve 1: a 2. A Failure, so Hurt equal to Hit, which is 1. This is a hidden place, so Tess’s Veil doesn’t raise Exposure.

Reading a stat block

Each threat below has the same lines. The three numbers in each stat are Easy · Standard · Hard. What it is and Plan say what it wants: the plan is complete if Doom fills. Signs are what the coven finds on leads. Weakness is what all the Clues reveal. The hidden place is where the coven finds it with half the Clues. The public place is where it comes, with the innocent in its grip, if Doom reaches the Showdown mark. The signature move is its 6 on the move table. Talks and acts is how to play it. If driven off is what it does when it gets away.

An episode made with the generator in Chapter 11 can give a threat a different plan and different places. Then use the rolled ones.

The Quiet Man “Thief of voices”

Defence
1 · 2 · 2
Danger
1 · 3 · 3
Hit
2 · 2 · 3
Toughness
3 × w · 3 × w · 4 × w

Easy · Standard · Hard. Defence +1 until the coven knows the Weakness.

What it is
A demon in a grey suit who steals voices.
Plan
Seven stolen voices to sing a door open.
Signs
Singers and speakers who open their mouths and make no sound. Old radios that pick up a choir of stolen voices at night. A man in a grey suit at the edge of every crowd, whose face nobody can describe. Salt lines on a doorstep, swept away.
Weakness
His own name spoken aloud, and salt.
Hidden place
The old radio tower in the Heights.
Public place
The Friday night market.
Signature move
Static: attack two different characters; the first one he hurts can’t cast on their next action. Targets: the most Hurt, then the next most Hurt, both standing (ties: roll a die among them); “first” follows that order. If only one character is standing, it is one attack. On their next action only, they can’t cast: no Power, no spell and no potion with that action, so they can’t lead or join the Unmaking with it. A guardian under Static can’t Mend or Carry, but it can Hold, since Hold isn’t casting. Static stops nothing else: Shield, defence rolls, Sight’s miss, someone else using a potion on them, and a Down witch joining the Unmaking all still work. Static ends after that action, or at the end of the next round.
Talks and acts
He never uses his own voice. He speaks in the voices he has stolen, often one the listener loves. Polite, patient and quietly pleased with himself. He hates his name, and changes the subject when anyone gets close to it.
If driven off
He climbs back to the tower with the voices he has, and waits to collect the rest.

The Ash Widow “The bride who burned”

Defence
1 · 2 · 2
Danger
1 · 3 · 3
Hit
2 · 2 · 3
Toughness
3 × w · 3 × w · 4 × w

Easy · Standard · Hard. Defence +1 until the coven knows the Weakness.

What it is
A fire spirit in a burned wedding dress.
Plan
To burn the house where she died, with a bride inside.
Signs
Small fires that burn in perfect rings and never spread. The smell of smoke and orange blossom. Wedding invitations found charred in letterboxes. A woman in a grey-white dress seen in windows that are on fire.
Weakness
Water from the harbour, blessed by a coven.
Hidden place
The burned-out theatre on the Docks.
Public place
The harbour fireworks.
Signature move
Cinders: one attack on every standing character, at Hit 1. Targets: every standing character, the guardian included. Each defends on their own. Shield, Shelter and Sight’s miss each stop only one of these attacks, and a guardian who Shields defends twice: once for its own attack and once for the one it took.
Talks and acts
A crackling whisper, like paper catching. She asks everyone she meets if they are to be married. She is grief more than hate, but grief that burns. She never runs; she walks through the flames.
If driven off
She sinks into the ashes of the theatre and waits for the next wedding in Larkhaven.

The Mirror Thief “Wearer of faces”

Defence

Danger
1 · 3 · 3
Hit
2 · 2 · 3
Toughness
3 × w · 3 × w · 4 × w

Easy · Standard · Hard. Defence +1 until the coven knows the Weakness.

What it is
A thin creature that wears stolen faces.
Plan
A face to keep forever.
Signs
People whose reflections have gone, and whose friends no longer know them. Mirrors turned to the wall. The same person seen in two places at once. Handprints on the inside of shop windows.
Weakness
Its true reflection: make it look in an old mirror.
Hidden place
Behind the mirrors of a closed department store.
Public place
St Bride’s market hall.
Signature move
Borrowed Face: attack the witch with the most Magic left, with Hit 1 higher; on a Failure they also lose 1 Magic. The standing witch with the most Magic; ties: roll a die among them. If the guardian takes the attack, by Hold or by Shield, it loses 1 Ward on a Failure instead of the Magic.
Talks and acts
It wears the face of someone you trust and speaks in their voice, but it gets small things wrong: a name, a joke, which hand they write with. Vain and hungry. It turns away from old glass.
If driven off
It slips into the nearest mirror, wearing the last face it took.

The Night Nurse “The one who sits by the bed”

Defence

Danger
1 · 3 · 3
Hit
2 · 2 · 3
Toughness
3 × w · 3 × w · 4 × w

Easy · Standard · Hard. Defence +1 until the coven knows the Weakness.

What it is
A creature that feeds on the sleeping sick.
Plan
One more night of feeding before the moon turns.
Signs
Patients on the night wards who sleep and won’t wake. Cold grey handprints on blankets. A nurse nobody on the rota knows, seen at the end of a corridor at three in the morning. The smell of lilies. Old iron bed frames wrapped in cloth.
Weakness
Iron, and being called by the name of someone it killed.
Hidden place
The closed wing of Larkhaven General.
Public place
The hospital car park at shift change.
Signature move
Drain: one attack; if it does any Hurt, the threat clears 2 damage. Target: whoever has the most Hurt marked and is still standing (ties: roll a die among them).
Talks and acts
Soft, kind and endlessly patient. It calls everyone “dear”. It never hurries. It looks at the hurt the way a hungry person looks at food. It keeps the names of the ones it fed on, and doesn’t like to hear them.
If driven off
It lies down in an empty bed in the closed wing and sleeps until the moon turns again.

Vesper Crane “The witch who takes”

Danger
1 · 3 · 3
Hit
2 · 2 · 3
Toughness
3 × w · 3 × w · 4 × w

Easy · Standard · Hard. Defence +1 until the coven knows the Weakness.

What it is
A witch who stole other witches’ power.
Plan
The coven’s Book.
Signs
Other witches in the city who wake with their magic gone. Second-hand bookshops broken into, with only the old books on magic taken. A tall woman in a long coat reading in the library after it has closed. Torn-out pages that smell of burnt rosemary.
Weakness
The spell she broke to steal her power, read back to her.
Hidden place
The library’s locked basement.
Public place
The university graduation.
Signature move
Turned Spell: one attack with Hit 1 higher. Target: whoever has the most Hurt marked and is still standing (ties: roll a die among them).
Talks and acts
Charming, clever and sure she is right. She offers the coven a deal before any fight. She thinks power belongs to whoever can hold it, and calls the coven “children”. She studies their spells and throws them back.
If driven off
She disappears with everything she learned about the coven, and comes back better prepared.

The Drowned Choir “The village under the sea”

Defence
1 · 2 · 2
Danger
1 · 3 · 3
Hit
2 · 2 · 3
Toughness
3 × w · 3 × w · 4 × w

Easy · Standard · Hard. Defence +1 until the coven knows the Weakness.

What it is
Ghosts of a village the sea took.
Plan
One more voice for the choir.
Signs
Singing over the marsh at dusk, many voices as one. Wet footprints that lead into the sea, and none that come back. People humming an old song nobody taught them. At low tide, the top of a church tower showing above the mud.
Weakness
A bell from the drowned church, rung at low tide.
Hidden place
The drowned village in Saltmarsh at low tide.
Public place
The long pier at dusk.
Signature move
Lure: one attack; then the standing character with the lowest Heart rolls Heart against Danger (no Power fits it, it isn’t casting, and there’s no Hurt on a Partial or a Failure); on a Failure they lose their next action. The attack is aimed like a 4–5: whoever has the most Hurt marked and is still standing (ties: roll a die among them). It is an attack, so Hold, Shield, Shelter and Sight’s Surge work on it as usual. The Heart roll isn’t an attack, so they don’t stop it: under Hold, the attack goes to the guardian and the Heart roll still happens. The Heart roll can fall on the guardian too (ties for the lowest Heart: roll a die among them). The lost action is this round’s if they haven’t acted yet, or else next round’s. If this round’s action is already lost, a second Lure takes next round’s. A witch who lost their action can still join the Unmaking without one, like a Down witch.
Talks and acts
They never speak alone. They sing, all together, in old-fashioned words. They are lonely, not cruel, and they don’t understand what they take. A bell makes them fall silent and listen.
If driven off
The tide comes in and they sink back under the marsh, still one voice short, to wait for the next low tide.

Making your own threat

A new threat needs the same stat block and a little story. Don’t give it numbers of its own: its Defence, Danger, Hit and Toughness come from the label.

  • What it is: a demon, a spirit, a rogue witch, a cursed thing or a creature, in one line.
  • A plan: what it wants, and what the world looks like if Doom fills. In the rules, a full Doom always means the innocent is lost and the threat escapes.
  • A Weakness: something the coven can find out and bring to the fight. A thing and a way to use it works well: salt and a name, a bell rung at low tide.
  • Two places: a hidden place where it hides, and a public place where it would strike with a crowd watching. Give each two ways in and out.
  • Signs, a voice and a getaway: three or four signs for the leads, a line on how it talks and acts, and what it does if driven off.
  • A signature move about as strong as “attack two different characters”. The six above show the range: two attacks with a small extra, every character at Hit 1, one attack with a side effect. Keep it to one move. Say exactly who it affects, so you never have to choose.

Last, check the name. It shouldn’t belong to a famous book, film or show.
