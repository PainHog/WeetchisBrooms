Chapter 4

The Guardian

The lamp over the door of the Lantern House has never gone out. Something keeps it lit. Something keeps the household safe, too, and has done for longer than anyone living can remember.

What the guardian is

The guardian is a protective spirit, bound to the coven’s home by an old promise. It takes a human or an animal shape. It answers to no master: nobody gives it orders, not even the coven. It stays because of the promise, and because it cares about the people under its roof.

The guardian isn’t a witch. It has no Power, no Craft, no Life and no Magic. It has Ward instead, and five gifts.

Each coven has one guardian. With 3–5 players, one player may play it instead of a witch. If two players want it, roll a die each: the highest plays the guardian and the other plays a witch.

A guardian player counts as one of the coven’s characters for the Clues needed, for the potion limit and for how many attacks the threat makes. It doesn’t count as a witch: it adds nothing to the threat’s Toughness and doesn’t change the Unmaking’s Difficulty.

Why play the guardian

  • You decide who stays standing. You heal, you take hits meant for others, and you can make the threat come for you instead.
  • You save the innocent. You can carry a freed innocent right out of the fight.
  • You make the whole coven better. Your Sense makes a lead land, and your Ward adds dice to anyone’s roll.
  • You are the strangest one at the table. You are old, you remember things, and you see the coven as your household.

The numbers allow for you. A coven with a guardian player does about as well as a coven with one more witch.

Ratings, Hurt and Ward

Put 3, 2 and 2 into Heart, Wits and Nerve, in any order. The guardian rolls like everyone else (Chapter 2).

The guardian has 5 Hurt boxes and starts each episode with 5 Ward. Spend Ward on gifts (1 each, except Hold), on helping someone else’s roll (1 die each) or on your own rolls (1 die each, inside the +2 limit). Unspent Ward is lost at the end of the episode.

The gifts

Using a gift counts as casting, except Hold: it costs no Ward and uses no magic. So in a public showdown, a round in which the guardian uses Mend, Shield or Carry raises Exposure, but a Hold doesn’t (Chapter 7).

GiftWardTakesWhat it does
Mend1An actionClear 2 Hurt from someone it touches.
Shield1No action; once a roundTake an attack meant for someone near it. In a showdown, everyone is near.
Carry1An actionTake one person to a place it knows, such as the freed innocent out of a fight. It can’t carry the coven out of a showdown or past a threat.
Sense1No action; once a scene+1 die on someone else’s lead this scene, outside the +2 limit.
Hold0An actionRoll Nerve against the threat’s Defence. If it works, the threat’s next attack must target the guardian. Not casting.

A few details:

  • Shield: the attack becomes the guardian’s, so the guardian rolls to defend against it (Chapter 7).
  • Carry: an innocent still in the threat’s grip can’t be carried.
  • Hold: “works” means a Partial or better, and a Partial still costs 1 Hurt. The Storyteller still rolls the d6 for the attack Hold has aimed, because a 6 still brings the signature move or takes the innocent (Chapter 7).

What the guardian can and can’t do

In the investigation, the guardian takes one action per scene like everyone else: it can follow leads with Heart or Wits, research the Book (a lead using Wits), rest, or use Mend or Carry. Sense takes no action. In the showdown it can free and guard the innocent and study the threat like anyone, use its gifts, and help with Ward.

The guardian can’t

Cast spells, brew or use potions (it has no Craft), join the Unmaking or damage a threat. It never attacks. It can help the Unmaking’s roll with Ward, like any other roll.

When the guardian goes Down

When all 5 Hurt boxes are full, the guardian is Down. A Down guardian fades back to the home for the rest of the scene. In a showdown, that means the rest of the fight. From there it can’t help. It is back up when its Hurt drops below full, like anyone else, so in a later scene of the investigation it can rest.

That is the guardian’s true cost: if it goes Down in a showdown, the coven loses its Shield, its Hold and its Ward for the rest of the fight.

Playing the guardian well

  • Spend your Ward. It’s gone at the end of the episode.
  • Shield the witch who would go Down. Your 5 boxes can take a hit that would drop them.
  • Hold before the threat’s turn. In round 1 the threat acts first, so a Hold in round 1 aims its first attack in round 2.
  • Carry the freed innocent out. Once they are gone, the threat can’t take them.
  • Sense the lead that matters most. Once a scene, on someone else’s lead. Its die is outside the +2 limit.
  • Help the Unmaking. You can’t join it, but a Ward die on the leader’s roll counts.
  • Don’t play a servant. You have no master. Play it old, odd and loyal.

Rook

The ready-made guardian is Rook (Heart 3, Wits 2, Nerve 2). In human shape, Rook is a very tall, very still person in a long grey coat, grey-eyed, who speaks little. In animal shape, a large black rook. Rook calls the witches “the household”. When Rook carries someone, the air folds like a page and they are somewhere else.

Example: Rook in a fight

A Standard showdown in a hidden place. The threat’s Defence is 2 (the coven knows its Weakness) and its Danger is 3, with Hit 2. The coven is Dana, Marco, June and Rook, four characters, so the threat makes 2 attacks a round. Rook has 3 Ward. Dana has 3 of her 4 Hurt boxes marked.

Rook holds. On the characters’ turn, Rook uses Hold: Nerve 2, plus 1 Ward for a die (2 Ward left), against Defence 2. Rook rolls 5, 4, 1: two successes, a Success. The threat’s next attack must target Rook.

The first attack. The Storyteller rolls the d6 anyway: a 3, not a 6, so it goes to Rook. Rook defends: Nerve 2 against Danger 3, rolling 4, 2. One success, when a Partial needs two: a Failure. Rook marks 2 Hurt (Hit 2).

The second attack. The d6 shows 5: whoever has the most Hurt marked. That is Dana, with 3. Even a Partial would put her Down. Rook uses Shield (1 Ward left) and takes the attack. Rook rolls 6, 4: two successes, a Partial. Rook marks 1 more Hurt, 3 of 5 in all. Dana stays standing.

If nobody plays the guardian

The guardian stays at home, guarding the house. It doesn’t take actions, roll or spend Ward, and it doesn’t count as one of the coven’s characters.

Calling the guardian

Once per episode, on any character’s turn, the coven may call the guardian for one Mend or one Carry. Calling takes no action and no roll, and it counts as casting.

Save the call for when it matters: Mend a Down witch back up in the showdown, or Carry a freed innocent out of the fight. A coven of 3 witches with no guardian player faces a threat with 3 more Toughness (Chapter 7).
