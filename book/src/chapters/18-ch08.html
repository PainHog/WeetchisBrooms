Chapter 8

Seasons

One episode is a good night. A run of them is a season: the same coven, the same house, the same city, getting older, stronger and more tangled up in each other’s lives. The threats remember the coven, too.

Stringing episodes together

A season is a run of episodes with the same coven. Each episode still works on its own. A season just adds a little carry-over between them.

Each episode starts fresh: 5 Magic for each witch, 5 Ward for the guardian, no potions (they spoil), and Doom, Exposure and Clues at 0. Hurt doesn’t carry over, except as a Scar. The Storyteller sets the episode’s numbers at its start (Chapter 6). They don’t change if someone joins or leaves during the episode.

Scars

Nobody dies from the dice. But a witch who is Down when the coven loses gets a Scar, unless the coven lost by retreating: they start the next episode with 1 Hurt marked. Once it is marked, it is ordinary Hurt, and rest, a Healing potion or the guardian’s Mend can clear it.

Play the Scar in the story: a burn that needs a dressing, a cough that won’t shift, a nightmare that comes back.

Growth

After every second episode of a season (the 2nd, the 4th, the 6th and so on), each witch raises one rating by 1. No rating can go above 4. Pick the rating your witch has been leaning on, or the one that let them down. The guardian doesn’t grow.

Book pages

When the coven destroys a threat, it gets a page in the Book. The next time the coven faces that kind of threat, it starts the episode with half the Clues, rounded up, so it can go to the threat as soon as the first scene ends. Pages last the whole season, and a new witch shares them (Chapter 5).

Returning threats

A threat that wasn’t destroyed comes back when the Storyteller chooses, with 2 more Toughness each time it escapes. That includes a threat that fled, one that escaped when Doom filled, and one the coven retreated from or fell to. A threat that has escaped twice comes back with 4 more.

Stronger threats

As the witches grow, so do the threats. After the 2nd and the 4th episodes, threats get 2 more Toughness per witch. So from the 3rd episode they have 2 more per witch, and from the 5th episode on they have 4 more per witch. After that they stop growing.

The same pattern goes on: one more raise every second episode, while the threats stay at +4 per witch. This is on top of the 3 more Toughness against a coven of 3 witches with no guardian player, and the 2 more each time a returning threat has escaped.

New and replacement witches

Players come and go during a season. A witch can leave the coven for good, and a new one can join. Make a new witch as in Chapter 3: no two witches share a Power, and a Life someone already has is rolled again.

A new witch

A new or replacement witch joins at the same growth as the coven’s lowest witch: they get as many raises as that witch has had. They share the coven’s Book, pages and all. They bring no Scars.

Give a new witch a tie to the coven that makes sense: a cousin who turned up with a suitcase, someone the coven saved last month, a friend who finally saw too much.

Romance and everyday life

The threat of the week is only half the show. The other half is the witches’ lives: jobs, families, money, friends who almost find out, and sometimes romance.

  • Romance is an optional story thread. There are no rules for it: no dice, no Magic, no track. Play it if the table enjoys it and skip it if not.
  • Mostly with people from everyday life: a colleague, a neighbour, a regular at the garage, the innocent from three episodes ago. A romance between a witch and the guardian isn’t part of the game’s usual setup.
  • Keep it to the table’s comfort. The game is for ages 13 and up. Fade to black is always there, and anyone can say “skip” or “fade” (Chapter 1).
  • Everyday life uses the rules you already have. Your Life adds a die when it fits, and it calls once each episode (Chapter 3). Everything else about everyday life is story.

The best seasons let the two halves crash into each other. The Storyteller can pick a Life call that lands on the worst possible night, or an innocent who turns out to be a witch’s new boss.

Running a season

  • The three ready-made episodes (Chapters 14–16) make a good start: Easy, then Standard, then Hard. After that, use the episode generator (Chapter 11).
  • Bring a returning threat back when the coven has almost forgotten it. It knows them now.
  • Keep a list of the people the coven has met. Let them come back as friends, problems, innocents or dates.
  • Give every witch’s Life a turn in the spotlight over the season.

Example: four episodes of a season

The coven is Dana, Marco and June, with Rook played by the fourth player.

Episode 1, Night Market (Easy). The Quiet Man is Broken but flees before the Unmaking works: a Partial. He wasn’t destroyed, so he will come back later with 2 more Toughness. No page.

Episode 2, Cinders (Standard). A Win. The Ash Widow gets a page in the Book.

After episode 2. Growth: Dana raises Nerve to 4, Marco raises Craft to 3 and June raises Wits to 4. From now on, threats have 2 more Toughness per witch.

Episode 3, Low Tide (Hard). The Drowned Choir has Toughness 4 × 3 = 12, plus 2 × 3 = 6 for the season: 18. Doom fills before the coven can Break the Choir: a Loss, with Marco Down. Marco gets a Scar, and the Drowned Choir will come back later too.

Episode 4. A fifth player joins with Ruth Penhale (Wild). The coven’s lowest witches have had one raise, so Ruth gets one; she raises Nerve to 2. She shares the Book and its page on the Ash Widow, and brings no Scar. Marco starts with 1 Hurt. The Quiet Man returns in a Standard episode. With 4 witches, his Toughness is 3 × 4 = 12, plus 2 × 4 = 8 for the season, plus 2 for returning: 22. After this episode, every witch, Ruth included, raises another rating, and threats will have 4 more Toughness per witch.
