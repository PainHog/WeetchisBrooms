Chapter 11

Making Episodes

Part Three has three ready-made episodes. When you’ve played them, or want something new, roll one up. Six dice give you a threat, an innocent, a plan, two places and a twist. Half an hour of notes turns them into a night’s play.

What an episode needs

Every episode, ready-made or rolled, has the same parts:

  • an opening: the threat strikes;
  • an innocent: the person the threat wants;
  • a threat, with its stat block from Chapter 10;
  • a label and the Clues needed;
  • six leads: two talking, two searching, one Book research and one for Reveal, with what each kind of Clue tells;
  • a Life call for each Life in the coven;
  • a hidden place and a public place, each with two ways in and out;
  • one twist;
  • a closing.

Reading the rolls

  • Threat. Use its stat block, its signature move and its voice (“Talks and acts”). The rolled plan and places replace its own. Keep the kind of Weakness, but put it in this episode’s story: if the Drowned Choir’s bell hangs in the lighthouse in Low Tide, here it might be on the innocent’s piano. Move its getaway (“If driven off”) to the new places too.
  • A threat met before. If the coven has destroyed this threat before, this is another of its kind, and the Book has a page on it: the coven starts with half the Clues (rounded up). If it escaped before, it comes back with 2 more Toughness for each time it escaped. If both have happened, it gets both: the page, and the Toughness for each escape.
  • Plan. Plans are story: each is what a full Doom looks like. In the rules a full Doom always means the same thing: the innocent is lost and the threat escapes. Plan 4 is the one plan with a rules effect. If Doom fills with plan 4, the threat comes back with 4 more Toughness: 2 for escaping and 2 for feeding.
  • A witch’s relative and a witch’s Life is tied to the innocent: pick a witch someone is playing, and ask that player who the person is.
  • Somewhere the coven knows well: a place from a witch’s Life, or a café or park they love. Never the Lantern House itself: threats can’t come inside uninvited.
  • A second, weaker threat is helping: the helper is story only. It has no stats and never fights. It leaves signs and gives the leads something to find.
  • A reporter is following the coven: Exposure starts at 1 instead of 0.

When the rolls clash

A few rolls fight each other. Reroll one of the two, or read them like this:

  • Vesper Crane and twist 4 (the threat was once a witch). She still is one. Read it as: she was once a witch of the Lantern House, and some of the Book’s pages are in her hand.
  • Twist 4 with a demon, a creature or the Drowned Choir. It became what it is: a witch who made a bad bargain, or the one witch among the drowned.
  • Someone the threat once loved, with a very old threat or one that can’t love (the Quiet Man, the Night Nurse, the Drowned Choir). Make it someone it once wanted, spared or sang with, or the grandchild of someone it loved long ago.
  • Plan 5 (steals something from the coven). It still needs the innocent, as a key, a hostage or a way in. The theft is story: the coven keeps the Book in the rules (its pages and its +1 die).
  • A place that doesn’t suit the threat, such as the Ash Widow under the sea or the Drowned Choir on the radio tower. Give it a reason to be there: a burned wreck that shows at low tide, a hymn that comes in over the old radio.

From rolls to an episode

  1. Pick the label and work out the numbers from the table in Chapter 6. The lookup table in Chapter 9 has them worked out for each group: Toughness, Clues needed, attacks a round and the Unmaking’s Difficulty.
  2. Write the opening. The threat strikes someone near the innocent, or someone like them. Use one of the threat’s signs. Keep it short and strange: one scene, one image, one scream or one silence.
  3. Make the innocent a person. Give them a name, a job, something they want this week, and the reason the threat wants them.
  4. Write six leads. Two talking leads (Heart): people who saw something. Two searching leads (Wits): places with something to find. One Book lead: what the coven’s spellbook says, researched at home. One Reveal lead: something hidden that only magic shows. For each, write who or where it is and a line on what it tells.
  5. Place the two locations. The hidden place is where the coven finds the threat with half the Clues; the innocent isn’t there. The public place is where the threat comes, with the innocent in its grip, if Doom reaches 5. Give each two ways in and out: a door and a roof, a ramp and a fire escape.
  6. Decide when the twist comes out. Tie it to a lead or to a Doom number, so it lands in the middle of the investigation.
  7. Write the Life calls (below).
  8. Write the closing: one line each for a Win, a Partial and a Loss.

Also note what the threat does as Doom rises: a new victim at Doom 2, a move on the innocent at Doom 4. That gives every scene’s end something to show.

Don’t tie a number of Clues to each lead. The dice decide how many Clues the coven finds; the leads tell you what those Clues say. Then write three lines for the whole episode: what early Clues show (what is happening), what the coven learns at half (what the threat is and where), and what the last Clue gives (the Weakness).

Life calls

Each witch’s Life calls once per episode. Write a call for each Life in the coven, tied to this episode if you can, and number them as on the Life table in Chapter 3. Nobody rolls on this table: each witch gets the call for their own Life. A witch answers in any scene: it takes their action, needs no roll and gives 1 Magic. If they haven’t answered by the showdown, they lose 1 Magic (or take 1 Hurt if they have none). Here is a table for any episode:

d6LifeThe call
1StudentAn exam, a deadline or a group project that can’t wait
2NurseAn extra shift on a short-staffed ward
3Shop workerA late delivery, a stocktake, or the boss turning up
4TeacherParents’ evening, a school trip or a pupil in trouble
5MechanicA rush job for a customer who won’t take no
6Bus driverA night route nobody else will cover

A worked example: The Face in the Glass

Four players: June, Tess and Noor are the witches, and the fourth player plays Rook. The Storyteller rolls the six tables: 3, 6, 2, 5, 6, 4.

  • Threat (3): the Mirror Thief.
  • Innocent (6): someone the threat once loved.
  • Plan (2): opens a door for something worse.
  • Public place (5): a ferry, pier or station.
  • Hidden place (6): somewhere the coven knows well.
  • Twist (4): the threat was once a witch.

The numbers

Standard, w = 3, n = 4. Lead Difficulty 2. Defence 2 (3 until the Weakness is known), Danger 3, Hit 2. Toughness 9 (3 × 3; no extra 3, since a player plays the guardian). Clues needed 15 (4 × 4 − 1), half 8. Exposure 4 boxes. Doom 12, mark at 5. Two attacks a round (four characters). The Unmaking’s Difficulty is 3, or 5 without the Weakness. Signature move: Borrowed Face. Weakness: its true reflection (make it look in an old mirror).

The story

Sixty years ago a young witch called Mae Ardley lived at the Lantern House. She was afraid of growing old, and she found a spell to be seen forever. It worked too well. It pulled her into the mirrors, and over the years she became the Mirror Thief. Now it has found a door behind the glass, and it needs one face to open it: the face of the man who loved her. If the door opens, the Mirror Thief steps out for good, and it leaves the door open behind it.

The opening

On the last ferry of the night, a woman glances at the dark window and sees a stranger’s face looking back. Then her own reflection is gone. By morning her husband says he doesn’t know who she is.

The innocent

Walter Ashdown, 81, a retired ferry captain who still rides the evening crossing every day. The girl he was going to marry vanished in 1965, and he keeps her photo in his wallet. He is kind, stubborn and doesn’t believe in magic.

The leads

  • Talking (Heart): Walter, on the ferry. He tells of Mae, who stopped looking in mirrors the month before she vanished. Lately, faces from his past keep turning up: his late brother on the quay, his old first mate at the bar.
  • Talking (Heart): the drivers at the night-bus depot. A new driver nobody hired, seen in the washroom mirrors. Two drivers have lost their reflections. Tess’s Life fits here.
  • Searching (Wits): the ferry terminal. Lost property holds a hand mirror with fingerprints on the inside of the glass. The cameras show a figure walk into a window and not come out.
  • Searching (Wits): the victims’ homes. Every mirror is turned to the wall. Every victim is in an old photo with Walter: it is practising faces from his life.
  • Book research: the attic. Old pages in a hand nobody knows, signed “M. A.”: a spell to be seen forever, written by a witch of this house.
  • Reveal: a mirror the Mirror Thief passed through. For a moment the glass shows its true face, young and very old at once, and a door behind the glass, standing ajar.

What the Clues tell. Early Clues: people are losing their faces, something in the mirrors is copying faces from Walter’s past, and it wants him. At half (8): it is the Mirror Thief, it was once a witch called Mae, and it hides behind the mirrors at the night-bus depot. All 15: make it look at its true reflection in an old mirror, such as Mae’s own hand mirror, in a trunk in the attic.

As Doom rises. At Doom 2, a deckhand loses his face. At Doom 4, it visits Walter wearing his brother’s face.

The places

  • Hidden place: the night-bus depot on Ferry Road, where Tess drives from. The fight is in the old drivers’ washroom, a long wall of mirrors. Ways in and out: the drivers’ gate from the bus yard, and the fire door to the back lane.
  • Public place: the ferry terminal at the Harbour, as the evening crossing boards. Ways in and out: the passenger ramp, and the car deck.

The twist

When the coven follows the Book lead, or reaches half the Clues, tell Rook’s player that Rook knew her. Rook remembers Mae: how she laughed, and how she stopped. The player decides when Rook says so. It gives no Clues, but now the coven is fighting one of its own.

4TeacherA school trip on the evening ferry needs one more adult
5MechanicThe ferry company’s van has broken down, and they’ll pay double
6Bus driverThe depot is short of drivers, and Tess is asked to cover the last route

The closing

  • Win: Walter looks at the ferry window. For a moment a young woman stands beside him in the glass, smiling, and then she is gone. The coven writes the Mirror Thief’s page in the Book, next to Mae’s own.
  • Partial: if it fled, the Mirror Thief slips into the dark glass, wearing the last face it took. If Walter was lost, the ferry sails without him, and his photo of Mae is found on the deck.
  • Loss: if Doom filled, the door behind the mirrors opens. Walter is gone, and in the morning every mirror in the depot is cracked from the inside. If the coven retreated or every witch went Down, the Mirror Thief slips back into the glass to try again. If Walter was still in the fight, it takes him with it.
