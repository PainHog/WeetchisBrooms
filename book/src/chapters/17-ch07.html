Chapter 7

The Showdown

Sooner or later the coven and the threat meet. It might be in the threat’s lair, on the coven’s terms. It might be in the middle of a crowd, with the innocent in its grip. Either way, only the coven together can finish it.

Where it happens

If the coven went to the threat, the showdown is in a hidden place. The innocent isn’t there, and casting doesn’t raise Exposure. If the threat came to the coven, the showdown is in a public place. The innocent is there, in its grip, and every round with casting raises Exposure (Chapter 6).

Everyone keeps their Hurt, Magic, Ward and potions from the investigation.

Rounds

The showdown is played in rounds. The whole showdown counts as one scene for anything that works once per scene, such as a Surge.

Order of a round

Round 1: the threat’s attacks; the characters’ actions; the innocent (still in its grip? they’re lost); Exposure; Doom +1.

Later rounds: the characters’ actions; the threat’s attacks; Exposure; Doom +1.

At the start of any round, the coven may retreat (below).

Actions

In each round, every character takes one action, in any order they like, or does nothing:

  • Attack the threat (below).
  • Cast a spell: Bind, Blast, Shelter, Hide or the Unmaking (Chapter 5).
  • Free the innocent, or guard them (below).
  • Study the threat: Wits against its Defence. It gives 1 Clue, so the coven can find the Weakness mid-fight.
  • Use a gift (the guardian, Chapter 4).

Some things take no action: helping a roll, using a potion, the guardian’s Shield, Sight’s miss Surge, and a Down witch joining the Unmaking. Potions are used while the characters act, before or after your own action, never on the threat’s turn: one per character a round, and an attack takes at most one, the attacker’s own (Chapter 5).

A Down character takes no actions, uses no potions and can’t Surge, not even Sight’s miss. Someone else can use a Healing potion on them to bring them back up (Chapter 3).

Attacks and damage

To attack, roll Nerve, or Craft if you cast Blast. A Power that fits adds 2 dice: Force and Flame fit attacks. The Difficulty is the threat’s Defence.

If the attack works (a Partial or better), add: +1 for Blast, +1 once the coven knows the Weakness, +2 for Flame’s Surge and +2 for a Blasting potion. Mark the damage against the threat’s Toughness. Damage past its Toughness is lost. The guardian never attacks.

The Weakness

Until the coven knows the threat’s Weakness, its Defence is 1 higher for every roll against it. Once the coven knows it, attack rolls that work, Blast included, do 1 more damage. Potions don’t. The Unmaking is also 2 harder without it (below). Wild’s Surge can lower Defence, but no Difficulty is ever below 1 (Chapter 2).

Knowing the Weakness is enough. The coven has what it needs, such as the salt or the bell, as part of the story. Using it takes no action and no roll.

If the coven came in short, studying is how it catches up. Each study that works gives 1 Clue, a Critical gives 2, and Sight’s Surge adds 1 more.

The threat

A threat has four numbers: Defence (the Difficulty to hurt it), Danger (the Difficulty to defend against it), Hit (the Hurt it does on a failed defence) and Toughness (the damage that Breaks it).

The threat’s attacks

It makes 1 attack a round against 3 characters, and 2 against 4 or 5. A guardian counts only if a player plays it.

It loses at most one attack a round, whatever the cause (Bind, Force’s Surge, a Binding potion). Any more are wasted. Sight’s miss isn’t a lost attack.

Against a coven of 3 witches with no guardian player, it has 3 more Toughness.

The threat’s turn

For each attack, the Storyteller rolls a d6 to see what the threat does.

d6The threat…
1–3attacks whoever hurt it last. If nobody has, or they’re Down, use 4–5.
4–5attacks whoever has the most Hurt marked and is still standing. Ties: roll a die among them.
6uses its signature move (one with none attacks two different characters). But if the innocent is there and free, it goes for them instead: it attacks their guard, or takes them if nobody is guarding them.

Taking the innocent isn’t an attack, so Hold can’t stop it. That is why the Storyteller rolls the d6 even for an attack Hold has aimed. If it shows 6, the signature move still happens (or the innocent is taken). Hold only aims attacks: if the move attacks, the guardian is its first target, and Hold is used up. If it attacks the innocent’s guard, Hold sends that attack to the guardian instead, and Hold is used up. Taking an unguarded innocent isn’t an attack, so Hold stays for the next attack. Any part of a move that isn’t an attack still happens: under Hold, Lure’s attack goes to the guardian, and its Heart roll still happens. A move with no attack in it leaves Hold for the next attack. Each threat has its own signature move, and Chapter 10 says whom it targets. Every signature move picks only standing characters.

Defending

The target rolls Nerve against the threat’s Danger. A Power that fits adds 2 dice: Sight and Veil fit defending. That is casting, so in a public place it counts toward Exposure. A defence roll isn’t an action, so it takes no Surge.

That is all the Hurt the roll does. You can add dice with Magic or helpers as usual. Four things keep an attack off someone: Shelter (no roll, no Hurt), the guardian’s Shield (it takes the attack and defends itself), Sight’s Surge (the attack misses) and guarding the freed innocent (you take the attack instead of them). If Shield and Sight’s Surge could both be used on the same attack, Shield is chosen first.

The innocent

The innocent is only in public showdowns. In a hidden place they aren’t there.

In its grip

The threat starts the showdown with the innocent in its grip. While they are, nobody can guard or carry them.

Free them: roll Nerve against Defence (Force fits). Only a Success frees them. A Partial doesn’t, and still costs 1 Hurt.

If they are still in its grip at the end of round 1, they’re lost.

Once they’re free, if the threat takes them, they’re lost. A Broken threat lets go.

To guard a freed innocent, use your action. Until the end of the threat’s next turn, if it goes for them, it attacks you instead. It ends if you go Down. The guardian can also carry them out of the fight (Chapter 4). Once they’re gone, the threat can’t go for them.

In round 1, keep trying until someone frees them. A Partial isn’t enough.

Public showdowns and Exposure

In a public place, every round in which anyone was casting raises Exposure by 1, once per round, at the Exposure step. Powers, spells, potions and gifts all count, except the guardian’s Hold, which isn’t casting. Casting Hide doesn’t raise it either.

  • One caster or four, it’s the same 1 Exposure. Bunch your casting into the same rounds.
  • In a quiet round, leave your Power off.
  • Veil’s Surge, cast with Hide, hides all the coven’s casting for this round and the next.
  • Once the coven is Seen, each further rise becomes Doom +1 instead, until the threat is Broken (Chapter 6).

Broken

When the threat’s Toughness is full, it is Broken. It makes no more rolls or moves, so it doesn’t attack. Doom stops rising. It lets go of the innocent. At the end of the next round it flees, unless the coven unmakes it first.

The Unmaking

The Unmaking

Only against a Broken threat. One standing witch leads, with Craft, as their action, and pays 1 Magic.

Every other witch may join for 1 Magic. Each one adds 2 dice, outside the +2 limit. A standing witch joins as their action, so they must not have acted this round. A Down witch joins without one, and so does a witch who lost their action to Lure.

The Difficulty is the number of witches in the coven, +2 without the Weakness.

Only a Success destroys the threat. On a Partial or a Failure it isn’t destroyed and nobody takes Hurt. Try again next round if it’s still there.

The Unmaking is a spell, so it is casting. In a public place, every try counts toward the round’s Exposure, even a failed one.

The leader can still add up to +2 dice of their own: their Magic, or helpers, including the guardian’s Ward. The guardian can’t join.

Timing. You have the rest of the round the threat Breaks in, and all of the next round. If most of the coven has already acted, try now with whoever is left: a failed try costs no Hurt, only Magic (and in a public place it counts as casting). Or wait for the next round, when every standing witch can join. Either way, the next round is your last chance.

The Weakness matters here most. In a coven of three witches, a leader with Craft 2, two joiners and +2 dice make 8 dice. Against Difficulty 3 that works about 86% of the time. Without the Weakness it’s Difficulty 5: about 36%.

Retreat

At the start of any round, the coven may retreat. Everyone leaves together. The episode ends at once, and it is a Loss. The threat wasn’t destroyed, so it comes back later with 2 more Toughness (Chapter 8). An innocent still in the fight is lost. Nobody gets a Scar for a retreat: that is what retreating saves when a fight is going badly. It never turns a bad fight into anything better than a Loss.

How it ends

The episode ends at once, and the rest of the round doesn’t happen, when:

  • the threat is destroyed;
  • the threat flees;
  • Doom fills;
  • every witch is Down at the end of a showdown round (a standing guardian doesn’t count);
  • the coven retreats.

At the end of a round, “every witch is Down” is checked before a Broken threat flees. If both happen, it’s a Loss.

On a retreat, or when every witch is Down, an innocent still in the fight is lost. When Doom fills, the plan is complete and the innocent is lost. Either way, an innocent already carried to safety isn’t lost, but the result is still a Loss.

ResultWhat happened
WinThe threat was destroyed, and the innocent is safe.
PartialThe threat fled, or it was destroyed but the innocent was lost.
LossDoom filled, every witch was Down, or the coven retreated.

If the coven is Seen, the result drops one step: a Win becomes a Partial and a Partial becomes a Loss. A Loss stays a Loss.

Scars

Nobody dies from the dice. But a witch who is Down when the coven loses, except by retreating, gets a Scar: 1 Hurt at the start of the next episode (Chapter 8).

Fighting well

  • Keep 2 Magic for the Unmaking: 1 to join, and 1 for a die or a second try. Spend the rest. Unspent Magic is lost.
  • Short of the Weakness? Study first. Every later attack and the Unmaking get easier.
  • Break it early in the round. Let one witch land the last blow first, so the others still have their actions to lead or join the Unmaking.
  • Watch the 4–5 result. The most-hurt character draws the attacks. Shelter, Shield or heal them before they drop.
  • Use your potions. They take no action, and they spoil at the end of the episode anyway.

A worked showdown

This follows on from the example in Chapter 6. Every number is checked against the rules.

The Ash Widow: setting up

A Standard episode, in a hidden place: the burned-out theatre on the Docks. The coven is Dana (Force), Marco (Flame), June (Sight) and Rook, played by the fourth player. Four characters, so the Ash Widow makes 2 attacks a round. Three witches, so her Toughness is 3 × 3 = 9 (no extra 3, because a player plays the guardian). Danger 3, Hit 2. Defence 2, but 3 until the coven knows the Weakness. The Unmaking will be Difficulty 3, or 5 without the Weakness.

Clues 13 of 15. Doom 4. Exposure 1 of 4. No potions.

Dana: Nerve 3, Hurt 1, Magic 6. Marco: Nerve 3, Craft 2, Hurt 0, Magic 6. June: Wits 3, Craft 2, Hurt 0, Magic 4. Rook: Nerve 2, Wits 2, Hurt 0 of 5, Ward 4. Every Life call has been answered.

Round 1

The threat goes first. Attack one: the d6 shows 2. Nobody has hurt her yet, so use 4–5: the most Hurt marked is Dana, with 1. Dana defends: Nerve 3 against Danger 3. She rolls 5, 4, 2: two successes, a Partial. She marks 1 Hurt (2 of 4).

Attack two: the d6 shows 5, Dana again. A Failure would do 2 Hurt and put her Down. Rook uses Shield (3 Ward left) and takes the attack. Rook rolls 6, 3: one success, a Failure. Rook marks 2 Hurt (2 of 5).

June studies: Wits 3, plus 2 for Sight, against Defence 3. She rolls 6, 5, 4, 1, 1: three successes, a Success. 1 Clue (14).

Rook studies: Wits 2, plus 1 Ward for a die (2 Ward left), against Defence 3. Rook rolls 5, 4, 3: two successes, a Partial. It works with a cost: 1 Clue (15) and 1 Hurt (3 of 5). The coven has all the Clues and knows the Weakness: harbour water, blessed by a coven. From now on Defence is 2, and attacks that work do 1 more damage.

Marco attacks with fire and Surges: 2 Magic first (4 left). Nerve 3 plus 2 for Flame, against Defence 2. He rolls 6, 5, 2, 2, 1: two successes and one 6, a Success. Damage: 2, +1 for the Weakness, +2 for the Surge = 5. Toughness 5 of 9.

Dana attacks with Force and Surges: 2 Magic (4 left). Five dice against Defence 2: 4, 4, 3, 1, 1. Two successes, a Success. Damage 2 + 1 = 3, Toughness 8 of 9, and the Surge means the threat loses its next attack.

The innocent: not here. Exposure: a hidden place, so no rise. Doom rises to 5. The Showdown mark only matters in the investigation.

Round 2

The characters act first now. The coven wants the Ash Widow Broken before the others act, so Marco goes first.

Marco attacks, with no Surge left (one per showdown). Five dice: 5, 3, 3, 2, 1. One success, a Partial. Damage 1 + 1 for the Weakness = 2, and Marco marks 1 Hurt. Toughness is 9 of 9; the extra point is lost. She is Broken. She stops attacking, Doom stops rising, and she will flee at the end of round 3.

June leads the Unmaking: Craft 2, 1 Magic (3 left). Dana hasn’t acted, so she joins as her action: 1 Magic (3 left), +2 dice. Marco has acted, so he can’t join this round. June adds 1 of her own Magic (2 left) and Rook helps with 1 Ward (1 left): +2, the limit. Six dice against Difficulty 3: 5, 4, 3, 2, 1, 1. Two successes, a Partial. She isn’t destroyed, but nobody takes Hurt.

The threat’s attacks: none; she is Broken. Exposure: no rise. Doom: stays at 5.

Round 3

The last chance. June leads again: 1 Magic (1 left). Dana joins (2 left) and Marco joins (3 left): +4 dice. June spends her last Magic on a die, and Rook its last Ward: +2. Eight dice against Difficulty 3: 6, 5, 4, 4, 3, 2, 1, 1. Four successes, a Success. The Ash Widow is destroyed, and the episode ends at once.

The result. The threat was destroyed, and Hannah was never in danger: a Win. Exposure is 1 of 4, so the coven isn’t Seen and the Win stands. The Ash Widow gets a page in the Book.

Look at what made it work. Studying first found the Weakness, which made every later roll easier and cut the Unmaking from Difficulty 5 to 3. Rook’s Shield kept Dana standing. And everyone kept enough Magic to try the Unmaking twice.

Example: freeing the innocent

An Easy episode, Night Market. Quinn (Voice), Tess (Veil) and Noor (Wild) stayed one more scene to find all 9 Clues, so the Quiet Man came to them at the Friday night market with Grace Ellery in his grip. Three characters, so 1 attack a round. Defence 1, Danger 1. Toughness 3 × 3 = 9, plus 3 against 3 witches with no guardian player: 12. Exposure is 2 of 5.

The threat goes first. The d6 shows 4: the most Hurt marked is Tess, with 1. Veil fits defending, but using it would be casting, so Tess leaves it off. Nerve 3 against Danger 1: 4, 2, 1, a Success. No Hurt.

Tess frees Grace: Nerve 3 against Defence 1. She rolls 5, 2, 1: a Success. Grace is free.

Quinn guards Grace. No roll. Until the end of the Quiet Man’s next turn, if he goes for her, he attacks Quinn instead.

Noor casts Blast: 1 Magic, Craft 3 against Defence 1. Wild doesn’t fit attacks, but 3 dice beat her Nerve 1. She rolls 5, 4, 1: a Success. Damage 2, +1 for Blast, +1 for the Weakness = 4. Toughness 4 of 12.

The innocent: free, so not lost. Exposure: Noor cast, so it rises to 3. Doom rises by 1.

Because Noor cast anyway, Tess could have used Veil on her defence for no extra Exposure. But the threat acted first, before anyone knew. Plan your casting rounds together.
