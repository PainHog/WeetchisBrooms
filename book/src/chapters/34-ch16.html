Chapter 16

Episode 3: Low Tide

Storyteller only

This chapter is for the Storyteller. Players shouldn’t read it before they play it: the fun is in finding out.

Two hundred years ago, the sea swallowed a village in Saltmarsh, church and all. Its people still sing under the mud, and their song is missing one voice. Now the choir is calling for it, and the voice it wants belongs to a twelve-year-old boy. This is a Hard episode: expect witches to go Down.

The opening

Read this aloud, then ask the players why their witches were on the pier at dusk.

Dusk on the long pier. The tide is coming in fast across the sands, and the sky over Saltmarsh is the colour of a bruise. Somewhere out on the marsh, voices begin to sing: an old hymn, slow and sweet, in parts, many voices as one. Three strangers walk past the arcade, down the lifeboat ramp and into the sea, singing along. Up to their knees. Their waists. Their shoulders. The lifeboat crew runs. They pull all three out, choking and shivering. None of them remembers walking in. And far out over the marsh, where nobody is standing, the singing goes on.

Then tell each witch what their Life wants from them this episode (the Life calls table below).

The innocent: Kit Marlow

Kit is twelve. He sings in the choir at Hillside School and has the solo at the winter concert. He lives with his mum and his gran in a cottage on the edge of Saltmarsh. He is shy with adults, sharp with other children, and he hums all the time. His family is descended from the only child who got out of the drowned village alive.

If Doom reaches the Showdown mark, the choir comes for him on the long pier at dusk. If Sam Abernathy is in the coven, Kit is in Sam’s choir at Hillside School.

The threat: the Drowned Choir

Its stat block is in Chapter 10. The choir is the ghosts of Old Saltby, a village the sea took in the great storm of 1824. Its church, St Agnes, went down with it. The ghosts never speak alone. They sing, all together, in old-fashioned words. They are lonely, not cruel, and they don’t understand what they take.

  • Plan: one more voice for the choir.
  • Weakness: a bell from the drowned church, rung at low tide. The small bell of St Agnes hangs in the lighthouse.
  • Hidden place: the drowned village in Saltmarsh at low tide.
  • Public place: the long pier at dusk.
  • Signature move (on a 6): Lure: the character with the lowest Heart rolls Heart against Danger; on a Failure they lose their next action. If the innocent is there and free, it goes for him instead.

Go to it, or wait?

At the end of each scene, Doom +1. If Doom has reached 5, it’s too late: the choir comes to the coven on the long pier at dusk, with Kit in its grip. If not, and the coven has half the Clues, it knows what the choir is and where it hides, and may go to the drowned village (this takes no scene). Kit isn’t there.

Leads

Here are six leads to offer. Each says where it happens, the roll, and which Powers fit. Rolls are against the Lead Difficulty (2). A success gives 1 Clue, a Critical 2, a Partial 1 with a cost.

  • Use the What and where answer while the coven has fewer than half the Clues. Use the Weakness answer once it has half or more.
  • Players can come back to a lead. Show them something new there each time.
  • When the coven has all the Clues, tell them the whole Weakness plainly, whichever lead gave the last one.
  • Out in the city, a cost is Exposure +1 if the witch was casting (a Power or Reveal), or else 1 Hurt. Anyone else the coven talks to is a talking lead too.

1. The three who walked in (talking)

Where: a ward at Larkhaven General, where the three are kept in overnight: Ray Pascoe, a ferry hand; Nell Ahern, a birdwatcher; and Dev Sood, a student. A nurse’s Life might fit here. Roll: Heart. Powers that fit: Sight, Voice, Wild.

What and where: Each of them heard singing from the Saltmarsh reeds, “from under the ground”, and found themselves walking along the shore towards a church tower that shows above the mud at low tide. They are still humming a hymn none of them knows. Ray says, “They tried me and spat me back. I wasn’t the one they wanted.”

Weakness: Nell says the singing has stopped once before, years ago, just for a breath. A bell rang out over the bay at the lowest tide, from the harbour side.

2. Kit and his gran (talking)

Where: the Marlow cottage on the edge of Saltmarsh. Roll: Heart. Powers that fit: Sight, Voice, Wild.

What and where: Kit has been slipping out to the pier at dusk all week “to listen”. He sings the hymn perfectly and has never learned it. His gran says the family came out of Old Saltby, the village under the marsh: one little girl, carried out of the flood in 1824, the only one the sea gave back.

Weakness: Gran remembers her own gran saying, “The lighthouse men took the little bell from the church. When it rings at low water, the old ones sleep.”

3. The long pier (searching)

Where: the lifeboat ramp and the beach under the pier, the Harbour. Roll: Wits. Powers that fit: Sight, Veil, Wild.

What and where: Three sets of footprints, exactly in step, come along the shore from Saltmarsh, three miles away. There is reed seed and black marsh mud on the ramp, not sand. On a post under the pier, someone has scratched SALTBY over and over, at the height of a child.

Weakness: The tide board on the pier shows that this week the lowest tides fall at dusk. The old men fishing off the end say the singing always stops at slack water, “when the bell used to go”.

4. The lighthouse (searching)

Where: the lighthouse on the harbour wall, now a little museum that keeps the old keepers’ logs. Roll: Wits. Powers that fit: Sight, Veil, Wild.

What and where: The keeper’s log for 1824: Great storm. Saltby gone under the sea. Bells heard ringing under water all night. An old chart shows the village under the Saltmarsh mud, uncovered only at the lowest tides.

Weakness: The log for 1825: Brought up the small bell of St Agnes from the wreck and hung it for our fog bell. Rang it at low water. The singing stopped. The bell still hangs by the lamp room.

5. The Book (research at home)

Where: the attic of the Lantern House. Roll: Wits with +1 die (the Book), with no Exposure; one character per scene. Powers that fit: Sight.

What and where: A page on drowned choirs. They sing for a voice they lack, and draw others in to fill the gap, then let them go, because only a voice of their own blood will finish the song. They rise where they drowned, at the lowest water.

Weakness: Ring a bell of their own church at the lowest water, and the song breaks.

6. The birdwatchers’ hide (Reveal)

Where: the hide on stilts in the Saltmarsh reed beds. Nothing ordinary shows here, so Heart and Wits find nothing. Roll: Reveal (Craft, 1 Magic); a success gives 1 more Clue. It is casting, so a cost is Exposure +1. Powers that fit: Sight.

What and where: Reveal shows the drowned village under the reeds, lit as if by candles: streets, cottages, the church, and the choir standing in rows in the nave, mouths open, waiting. One place in the front row is empty, at a child’s height.

Weakness: In the church tower, Reveal shows the bell frame with one place empty, where the smallest bell should hang. A thin line of light runs from it across the bay to the lighthouse.

The hidden place: the drowned village

Old Saltby lies under the Saltmarsh mud and shows only at the lowest tides, which fall at dusk this week. The coven goes to it with half the Clues; going takes no scene. Nothing here needs a roll to get past. If the coven retreats, everyone leaves together by any way out. Kit isn’t here.

  • The causeway. A raised path of old stones from the birdwatchers’ car park out across the mud. The sea covers it at high tide.
  • The reed beds. Tall reeds and channels of black water. The hide stands on stilts, with a flat-bottomed boat tied below it.
  • The village street. The stumps of cottage walls in the mud, a well with no water in it, a lane of cobbles.
  • The churchyard. Leaning stones, half buried, crusted with shells.
  • St Agnes’s church. Roofless walls round the nave, where the choir stands in rows. The tower is still whole, with an empty bell frame at the top.
  • The mudflats. Open, shining mud all the way to the sea and round the bay to the beach under the long pier.

Ways in: the causeway from the car park; the reed channel by boat, from the hide; along the sea wall from the Harbour side. Ways out: the same three, and the long walk across the mudflats to the beach under the long pier.

As the coven arrives, the singing stops. Every face in the choir turns. Then they sing again, and the song is aimed at the coven. As Doom rises, so does the water.

The public place: the long pier at dusk

If Doom reaches the Showdown mark, the choir comes to the pier at dusk, when it is busiest. In a public place, every round in which anyone was casting raises Exposure by 1 (once per round). Nothing here needs a roll to get past.

  • The gates and the arcade. Bright lights, slot machines and a candyfloss stall at the landward end.
  • The boardwalk. Planks over the water, benches, and people fishing off the rail.
  • The halfway café. Closed for the evening, with a long glass front.
  • The pier head. The widest part, with a bandstand and coin telescopes.
  • The lifeboat ramp. A slipway from the pier head down into the sea.
  • The beach steps. Iron stairs from the boardwalk down to the sand under the pier.

Ways in and out: the gates from the promenade; the beach steps; the lifeboat ramp; the ferry landing at the pier head.

In its grip: Kit stands at the bottom of the lifeboat ramp, knee-deep in the sea, singing. The choir stands round him in the water, holding his hands. To free him, roll Nerve against Defence; only a success frees him. If he is still in its grip at the end of the first round, he is lost. Once he is free, Rook (or the coven’s call) can Carry him to a place it knows, such as the Lantern House.

The twist

Kit doesn’t want to be saved. All week, the choir has sung his name, and it sounds like family. The empty place in the front row is his, and he knows it. If the coven talks to him, Kit asks them to leave the choir alone: “They’re not hurting anyone. They’re just lonely.”

The twist comes out on Kit’s lead, or in the showdown, when Kit doesn’t struggle and asks the coven to stop. Let the players decide how to reach him: what to say, and what to promise. The twist changes no rules: freeing him still takes a success, and he can still be guarded and carried once he is free.

Endings

Win (the choir is destroyed, and Kit is safe). The small bell of St Agnes rings out over the low water. The song breaks in the middle of a note. The choir falls silent and listens, and one by one the ghosts lie down under the mud. The marsh is quiet. Kit sings the solo at the winter concert, and cries a little afterwards, and doesn’t say why.

Partial (it fled, or it was destroyed but Kit was lost). If it fled, the tide comes in and the choir sinks back under the marsh, still one voice short, to wait for the next low tide. Kit’s gran locks the doors every evening at dusk. If it was destroyed but Kit was lost, he walked into the water with the choir as it went. The lifeboat searches all night. Whether Kit can be found is a story for another episode.

Loss (Doom filled, every witch Down, or the coven retreated). If Doom filled, its plan is complete: Kit is lost and the choir escapes. Every evening at dusk, people on the pier hear the singing, and among the voices is a boy’s. If every witch went Down or the coven retreated, the tide covers Old Saltby and the singing goes on, every dusk, still one voice short.

Seen (the result drops one step; a Loss stays a Loss). Half the pier filmed it: the ghosts in the water, the boy on the ramp, the people doing impossible things. The lifeboat crew gave interviews. Or, if the coven was Seen during the investigation, Nell Ahern saw a witch cast at the hide and has told every birdwatcher in Larkhaven.

Carry-over. If it was destroyed, the Drowned Choir gets a page in the Book (next time, start with half the Clues). If it wasn’t, it comes back later with 2 more Toughness. A witch who is Down when the coven loses gets a Scar: 1 Hurt at the start of the next episode.

Closing scene

The night of the winter concert at Hillside School. Give each player a short Life scene first: who they let down this week, and how they made it up. Then go to the concert. If Kit is safe, he sings the solo, and for one line the hall hears something old in his voice. If he isn’t, his place in the front row stays empty. End at the lighthouse, where somebody has to decide whether the small bell goes back on its hook, or comes home to the Lantern House.
