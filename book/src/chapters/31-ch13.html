Chapter 13

The Coven and the Lantern House

Here is a coven ready to play: six witches, one for each Power, and Rook, the guardian. This chapter also shows the house they share and the city around it. Use them for your first night, or as examples when you make your own.

Using the ready-made coven

Each player picks one witch. One player may pick Rook instead. Copy the numbers onto a witch sheet or the guardian sheet at the back of the book.

  • The ratings already follow the rules: 3, 2, 2 and 1, with the 3 where the Power is used most.
  • Each witch starts every episode with 5 Magic and 4 empty Hurt boxes. Rook starts with 5 Ward and 5 empty Hurt boxes.
  • No two of them share a Power or a Life.
  • Any witch can be any gender. Change a name, a look or a pronoun if you like. Keep the numbers.

Each entry lists what the witch’s Life might fit. These are ideas, not promises: a Life adds 1 die only when the Storyteller agrees it fits.

The witches

Dana Okafor (she)

“Sit down. Let me look at that.”

Look: Tall, with short natural hair. Scrubs under a denim jacket, and a silver ring on her left thumb.

Personality: Steady and blunt. Twelve-hour shifts have taught her to stay calm and to have no time for nonsense.

Power: Force

She moves things with her mind. +2 dice when it fits: attacks and freeing.

Surge (2 Magic, once per scene, paid before she rolls): on a Partial or better, the threat loses its next attack.

Heart 2 · Wits 2 · Nerve 3 · Craft 1 · Hurt 4 · Magic 5

Life: Nurse. It might fit first aid, hospitals at night and calming frightened people.

Tie to the coven: She grew up next door to the Lantern House. She saw the lamp from her bedroom window every night of her childhood.

Hook: An old man on her ward, who never has visitors, keeps asking for “the girl from next door to the lamp”. He knows things about the house that nobody should.

Marco Rossi (he)

“I can fix that. Probably.”

Look: Stocky, with oil on his hands and his sleeves rolled up. A burn scar on his right forearm.

Personality: Loud, kind and quick to laugh. He would rather fix a problem with his hands than talk about it.

Power: Flame

He calls fire. +2 dice when it fits: attacks.

Surge (2 Magic, once per scene, paid before he rolls): on a Partial or better, 2 more damage.

Heart 2 · Wits 1 · Nerve 3 · Craft 2 · Hurt 4 · Magic 5

Life: Mechanic. It might fit engines, tools, cars and vans, and the back streets of the Docks.

Tie to the coven: He is a cousin of the family that owns the house. He was round fixing the boiler the day his Flame woke, and the scar is from that day.

Hook: One of his cousins has started talking about selling the Lantern House. Marco hasn’t told the others yet.

June Takeda (she)

“Hang on. I wrote that down somewhere.”

Look: Small, with round glasses and a yellow raincoat. She always carries a notebook.

Personality: Curious and a little nervous. She writes everything down, and she trusts the Book more than she trusts herself.

Power: Sight

Glimpses of what’s coming or what happened. +2 dice when it fits: leads, defending and studying.

Surge (2 Magic, once per scene, paid before she rolls): on a Partial or better, 1 more Clue. Or, with no action, before the defence roll, one attack aimed at anyone misses (this isn’t a lost attack).

Heart 2 · Wits 3 · Nerve 1 · Craft 2 · Hurt 4 · Magic 5

Life: Student. It might fit libraries, research, getting into the university after hours, and knowing what students are talking about.

Tie to the coven: She found the Book in a second-hand shop on University Hill. The next morning it was gone from her desk, and she followed the trail of it to the Lantern House attic.

Hook: The shop where she found the Book has opened again under a new owner, who keeps asking customers where it went.

Sam Abernathy (they)

“Let’s all take a breath, shall we?”

Look: Lanky, with curly red hair and a cardigan. A whistle on a cord round their neck.

Personality: Gentle, funny and stubborn. Sam hates using Voice on anyone and would rather talk people round the slow way.

Power: Voice

Words that sway minds. +2 dice when it fits: talking leads and Hide.

Surge (2 Magic, once per scene, paid before they roll): on a Partial or better, 1 more Clue, or Exposure −1.

Heart 3 · Wits 2 · Nerve 1 · Craft 2 · Hurt 4 · Magic 5

Life: Teacher at Hillside School. It might fit children and parents, schools, getting a room to listen, and speaking in public.

Tie to the coven: An old friend of Dana’s from school. Dana was the first person Sam told about their Power.

Hook: A pupil has started repeating, word for word, something Sam once said to them using Voice. Sam doesn’t know how long it will last.

Tess Lindqvist (she)

“Next stop. Mind the doors.”

Look: Broad-shouldered, with a blonde braid and a driver’s uniform. A pale streak runs through the braid.

Personality: Dry, patient and hard to rattle. She knows every street and every regular, and says little.

Power: Veil

She goes unseen and hides magic. +2 dice when it fits: defending, searching leads and Hide.

Surge (2 Magic, once per scene, paid before she rolls): on a Partial or better, no Exposure from casting for the rest of the scene, or in a showdown for this round and the next (a casting roll’s cost becomes 1 Hurt).

Heart 1 · Wits 2 · Nerve 3 · Craft 2 · Hurt 4 · Magic 5

Life: Bus driver. It might fit streets and routes, the city at night, the regulars on her bus, and keeping calm behind the wheel.

Tie to the coven: She inherited a room in the house from her gran. The pale streak appeared the first time she went unseen.

Hook: Her gran also left a locked trunk in the attic. The label, in her gran’s hand, says “For Tess, when she’s ready”.

Noor Haddad (she)

“The crows say we’re late.”

Look: A green headscarf and soil under her nails. A crow feather in her pocket.

Personality: Warm, practical and hard to surprise. Animals trust her before people do.

Power: Wild

She talks to animals, and grows and binds with plants. +2 dice when it fits: talking and searching leads, Bind and brewing.

Surge (2 Magic, once per scene, paid before she rolls): on a Partial or better, 1 more Clue or potion. Or, in a showdown, the threat’s Defence is 1 lower for everyone this round.

Heart 2 · Wits 2 · Nerve 1 · Craft 3 · Hurt 4 · Magic 5

Life: Shop worker at a garden centre. It might fit plants, gardens, animals, customers, and knowing what grows where in the city.

Tie to the coven: She answered an advert for a lodger and stayed. She has kept the walled garden ever since.

Hook: A crow follows her everywhere. When a threat is near, more crows come. This morning there were eleven on the Lantern House roof.

The guardian

Rook

“The household is in danger.”

Look: In human shape, a very tall, very still person in a long grey coat, with grey eyes. In animal shape, a large black rook. When Rook carries someone, the air folds like a page and they are somewhere else.

Personality: Patient, watchful and honest. Rook speaks little, never lies, and doesn’t always answer. It calls the witches “the household”.

Gifts (1 Ward each)

  • Mend (action): clear 2 Hurt from someone it touches.
  • Shield (no action, once a round): take an attack meant for someone near it (in a showdown, everyone is near).
  • Carry (action): take one person to a place it knows, such as the freed innocent out of a fight. It can’t carry the coven out of a showdown or past a threat.
  • Sense (no action): +1 die on a lead this scene, outside the +2 limit.
  • Hold (action, no Ward): Nerve against Defence; if it works, the threat’s next attack must target the guardian.

Heart 3 · Wits 2 · Nerve 2 · Hurt 5 · Ward 5

What Rook can’t do: Rook can free and guard the innocent and study the threat like anyone. It can’t cast spells, use potions, join the Unmaking or damage a threat. It can help with Ward. When Rook is Down, it fades back to the Lantern House for the rest of the scene (in a showdown, the rest of the fight) and can’t help from there.

Tie to the coven: Rook is bound to the Lantern House by an old promise. It was there before any of the witches, and it calls whoever lives in the house its household.

Hook: Rook has never said who made the old promise, or what happens if it is broken.

If nobody plays Rook: Rook stays home, guarding the house. Once per episode, on any character’s turn, the coven may call it for one Mend or one Carry. Calling takes no action and no roll, and counts as casting.

The Lantern House

The coven’s home is a narrow, three-storey house on a steep street in Old Town, squeezed between two others. A lamp hangs over the front door. It has never gone out, not in a storm and not in a power cut, and nobody has ever filled it.

Rook is bound to the house. In rook shape it sits on the chimney. In human shape it stands in the hall, so still that visitors take it for a coat stand until it moves.

What the house is for

Two investigation actions happen at home: researching the Book (a lead using Wits, +1 die, with no Exposure; one character per scene) and brewing potions. Threats can’t come inside uninvited. That is story only: the rules never put a showdown here.

Ground floor

  • The front room. Sofas that don’t match, a fireplace and a bay window onto the street. The coven meets here to plan, argue and eat takeaway.
  • The kitchen. A long scrubbed table, an old range and shelves of jars, half of them labelled in handwriting nobody living recognises. This is where potions get brewed.
  • The back door and the walled garden. Herbs, an apple tree and a bench under it. The walls are high and old. Noor keeps the garden; the crows keep Noor company.

First floor

  • Three bedrooms. In the ready-made coven, Tess has her gran’s room at the front, Noor has the lodger’s room at the back, and the small room over the stairs belongs to whoever needs it tonight. June has slept there more nights than she has slept at home.
  • The bathroom. A bath with clawed feet, pipes that knock, and a mirror that fogs over even when nobody has run the tap.

Attic

  • The Book on its lectern. A heavy book, written in many hands over many years. Every witch who has lived here added pages. Research happens here.
  • Old trunks. Clothes, letters, tools and keepsakes left by the witches who lived here before. Some are locked.
  • The round window. It looks down on the street and the lamp. From here, you can see who comes to the door before they knock.

Larkhaven

Larkhaven is an invented harbour city, set today. Fog in the mornings, ferries, steep streets, old money in the Heights and new flats on the old docks. The table shows where the ready-made episodes and threats happen.

Old Town

The oldest part of the city, built up the hill from the water. Cobbled streets too narrow for buses, little shops, and St Bride’s market hall with its iron roof. On Fridays the night market fills the lanes around the hall with food stalls, music and fairy lights.

The Harbour

Ferries come and go all day across the bay. The fish market opens before dawn. The long pier runs out into the sea, with an arcade at one end and a lifeboat ramp at the other. The lighthouse stands on the far arm of the harbour wall.

Saltmarsh

Flat, grey-green and quiet, east of the city. Reed beds, channels of black water and a birdwatchers’ hide on stilts. At the lowest tides, the walls of a drowned village show above the mud.

The Heights

Big houses behind high hedges on the hill above the city. At the top stand the observatory and the old radio tower, a steel mast that hasn’t sent anything out in fifty years.

University Hill

Students, bikes, cheap cafés and second-hand shops. The university library has a basement that is always locked, and nobody on the staff will say why.

The Docks

Once ships and warehouses; now new flats with glass balconies. The container yard still works through the night. In the middle of it all stands a burned-out theatre that nobody has managed to knock down.
