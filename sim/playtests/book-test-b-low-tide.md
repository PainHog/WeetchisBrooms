# Book Test B: rowdy table, Episode 3: Low Tide at Hard

- **Rules tested:** the rulebook source, v0.9 test edition: `book/src/chapters/*.html` (Chapters 1–8, 9–12, 13, 16 and the sheets and quick reference), with the rulings logged in `book/REVIEW.md` (up to ruling 87). Nothing else was used. Where the book is silent, the table made the ruling a table would most likely make, and it is logged as an issue (section 4).
- **Date:** 2026-10-30.
- **Table:** an experienced Storyteller and 5 experienced players: four ready-made witches from Chapter 13 and one player as Rook.
- **Episode:** Episode 3: Low Tide, Hard, played as a one-off (not as the 3rd episode of a season), from the opening to the closing scene, following Chapter 9.
- **Brief:** a rowdy, clever table. They split up, take risks, try to break things, push every track, use every ability and argue rules. Look hard for dominant tactics, free spends, skipped rolls and ways to dodge costs.
- **Dice:** every die came from a logged dice script (computer random numbers). 137 rolls in all, each with an ID (R001…). The full list is in section 7. Nothing was invented. Where a drill needed a 6 on the move table, the die was re-rolled until it showed 6, and every re-roll is in the list, marked "re-rolled until a 6".

**Result: a Win.** The coven found all 20 Clues by the end of scene 4, went to the drowned village at Doom 4, Broke the Drowned Choir in round 2 and unmade it in round 3 with its one and only try. Every witch ended on exactly 0 Magic, Rook went Down to the first attack of the fight, and Exposure ended at 1 of 4.

### Blocker

**1. Does the Lure roll cost Hurt on a Partial or a Failure?**
- **Where:** Ch 10 and Ch 16, Lure: "the character with the lowest Heart rolls Heart against Danger; on a Failure they lose their next action." Ch 2: "A Partial or a Failure comes with a cost… In a showdown: 1 Hurt." Ch 7 closes the general rule off for defence rolls only: "That is all the Hurt the roll does."
- **What the table had to decide:** whether Tess (3 of 4 Hurt, under Shelter) went Down on a Lure Partial (D2, R069). It came up three times (R069, R092, R103). Read literally, Chapter 2's cost applies to every roll, so a Lure Partial costs 1 Hurt and a Failure costs the action *and* 1 Hurt. The simulator plays it as no Hurt. The two readings give different results, and the strict one makes Lure worse than an attack. Also, Tess with Heart 1 and no spare Magic rolls 1 die against Danger 3 and can't reach a Partial, so her roll can't be won.
- **Ruling to use tonight:** Lure does only what it says: a Failure loses the next action; a Partial or a Failure costs no Hurt.
- **Suggested fix:** add to Lure in Ch 10, Ch 16 and the quick reference: "That is all the roll does: no Hurt on a Partial or a Failure. You can add dice as usual." Optionally state that a witch who loses her action to Lure can still defend, help and use potions.

## 5. What worked

- **The core roll and the costs.** Every lead, brew, attack and defence used the same roll and the same cost table. Nobody had to look anything up after scene 1. Leaving a Power off to turn an Exposure cost into Hurt came up in planning and made sense to everyone.
- **The "go now or one more scene" choice** was real. At the end of scene 2 they had half and stayed. Scene 4's argument (Lives against leads against Magic) was the best twenty minutes of the night.
- **Life calls bite.** With Magic this tight, answering or skipping a Life call decided who could lead the Unmaking. Tess skipped hers and arrived with 1 Magic.
- **"Keep 2 Magic" proved itself by being ignored.** The coven came in with exactly 1 Magic per witch and got exactly one Unmaking try. Everyone felt it.
- **The showdown order rules held up under pressure:** the threat acting first in round 1, Hold and a 6 with a guarded innocent (D3b), "every witch Down" checked before fleeing (D8), the Broken threat making no attacks, Doom stopping, Seen not adding Doom once Broken (D6), and a retreat (D10). Every one had an answer in the book.
- **Public showdowns cost something.** D6 showed one careless casting round turning a Win into a Partial.
- **Losses are real at Hard.** D5 (everyone Down after one bad round), D7 (Doom filling) and D8 all ended in a Loss from plausible positions.
- **Down witches still matter.** They help and join the Unmaking, so a witch going Down isn't the end of her night.
- **The Book research plus brew** gave Noor's player a strong, interesting job every scene.
- **The numbers checked out:** the 13- and 14-dice claims, Chapter 7's 86% / 36% example, the Hard table in Chapter 16, the season Toughness figures (24 and 26) and the Book page's half-Clues start.

## 6. How it felt

- **Was losing a real risk?** In the investigation, no: the dice were kind (8 Clues in scene 1, all 20 by scene 4, three Criticals) and Doom never pressed. In the fight, yes. Rook went Down to the first attack, Dana was on 3 of 4 after round 1's attacks, and the Unmaking was a single 75% shot with no Magic for a second. A failed roll there would have meant a Partial (it flees). The drills showed short, believable paths to a Loss: one bad round (D5), a slow fight (D7) and one careless choice (D8).
- **Were resources spent?** Fully. Every witch ended on 0 Magic, and Rook on 0 Ward. Four Surges in the investigation and one in the fight. Seven potions brewed (one lost to the limit), four used. The two Binding potions spoiled, because Force's Surge already did their job and only one attack can be lost a round. Exposure barely moved (1 of 4): with a Power, a city lead is 4–5 dice against 2, so only one casting roll in six had a cost. Hide was never needed in the main game.
- **Was there a dominant tactic?**
  1. **Noor researching the Book and brewing every scene:** two rolls for one action, giving 4 Clues and 7 potions over three scenes. The potion limit stops it stacking up, but drinking a Healing at home and refilling turns the free brew into free healing.
  2. **A Blasting potion on every attack.** +2 even on a Partial, so a Partial with a potion (4 damage) beats a plain Success (3). Round 1's three Partials did 10 damage.
  3. **In public, round 1 buys off the public place:** Wild's Surge with Bind for freeing, Veil's Surge with Hide to hide two rounds of casting, and Carry to remove the innocent (D1). Strong, but it cost 8 Magic and 3 Ward.
  4. **Shelter on the guardian with Hold and Shield (D4)** is the strongest defence found. It blanked a round, then failed the next. It has never been simulated (Issue 10).
  
  No line felt unbeatable, but 4 needs the simulator.
- **Free spends, skipped rolls, dodged costs:** no truly free spend was found. Sense is the cheapest (1 Ward, no risk). The skipped rolls were Shelter (by design) and Shield or Hold onto a Sheltered guardian (D4). A Heart 1 witch's Lure roll can't be won with one die (Issue 1). Costs were dodged in the usual intended ways: leaving a Power off, Veil's Surge, and resting in the investigation.
- **How long did it take?** About 2½ hours for the episode: 10 minutes of setup with the roll-off, 10 for the opening, about 90 for four scenes (arguments included), 25 for three rounds and 10 for the closing. That is inside Chapter 9's 2–3 hours, with a short fight (Chapter 9 expects about 4 rounds at Hard).
- **Did the guardian player have as much to do as a witch?** In the investigation, more: a lead and a Sense every scene, so two things to a witch's one. In the main fight, much less: Down to the first attack and out for the rest. In D1 Rook was the busiest character at the table (Shield, Sense, Carry and Mend in two rounds). It's swingy: the guardian's night depends on not arriving hurt and empty, and the book doesn't warn the player about that (Issue 19).
