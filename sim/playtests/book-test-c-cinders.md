# Book Test C: new players, Episode 2: Cinders at Standard

- **Rules tested:** the rulebook source, v0.9 test edition: `book/src/chapters/*.html` (Chapters 1–8, 9–12, 13, 15 and the sheets and quick reference), with the rulings in `book/REVIEW.md` up to ruling 105. Rulings 88–105 were checked and are in the chapters. Nothing else was used. Where the book is silent, the table made the ruling a table would most likely make, and it is logged as an issue (section 4).
- **Date:** 2026-10-30.
- **Table:** a Storyteller who has read the book once, and 4 players new to the game, with ready-made witches from Chapter 13. **No guardian player**, so the coven could call Rook once.
- **Episode:** Episode 2: Cinders (Chapter 15), Standard, played as a one-off, from the opening to the closing scene, following Chapter 9.
- **Brief:** sensible newcomers. They ask questions and use Lives, spells, Surges, potions, Powers and Hide when it makes sense, and sometimes make a poor choice. Push toward the public place if the story leads there.
- **Dice:** every die came from a logged dice script (computer random numbers), one d6 per die, each roll labelled and given an ID as it was made. 77 rolls in all: 2 at setup, 28 in the episode and 47 in the drills. The full list is in section 8. Nothing was invented, and no roll was thrown away. Where a drill needed a 6 on the move table, the die was re-rolled until it showed 6, and every re-roll is in the list.

**Result: a Win.** The coven found all 15 Clues by the end of scene 4. Then it chose to play a fifth scene so two witches could answer their Life calls, which brought the Ash Widow to the harbour fireworks with Hannah Price in her grip. Tess freed Hannah in round 1, the coven called Rook to carry her to the Lantern House, and Marco and June Broke the Ash Widow in round 1 with two Critical rolls. The Unmaking worked at the first try in round 2. Exposure ended at 1 of 4, so the coven wasn't Seen.

**Clean? No.** There was no blocker, but the table had to make up a ruling on showdown flow: whether the Ash Widow's Cinders is one attack or one attack on each character, which decides what Sight's miss, Shield and Hold do against it (Issue 1). It came up in the main game (R022) and again in the drills.

## 3. Drills

Fresh rolls, from set-up positions. Each drill says what it tests and what the book said.

### D1. The opening isn't a scene (ruling 93), no rolls
Played at the table during the opening (section 2), with these questions:
1. **"Does the opening count as scene 1? Does Doom go up?"** No. Chapter 9: "The opening isn't a scene: nobody takes an action or rolls." Doom rises only at the end of a scene. Clear.
2. **Theo: "Marco's already at the garage with the wedding car. Can he answer his Life now?"** No. Answering takes your action in a scene (Chapter 3), and the opening has no actions. Clear.
3. **Callum: "Tess went unseen in front of the fire crews. Is that Exposure?"** No roll, so no cost. Clear.
4. **Jas: "Quinn calmed the families with Voice. Do we get a Clue for that?"** No: Clues come only from rolls. It becomes Quinn's first lead (she followed the families to the rest centre in scene 1, R003). Clear.
5. **Theo, at scene 1: "Do I have to do the lead I said in the opening?"** Chapter 15: "What they wanted to do becomes their first lead in scene 1." It reads like a lock. The table treated it as an offer, and Marco answered his Life instead. **Issue 4.**

**Verdict:** the rule worked. Four players wanted to act, and all four were turned into scene 1 leads without an argument.

### Confusing

**1. Cinders: one attack, or one attack on each character? It decides what Sight's miss, Shield and Hold do.**
- **Where:** Ch 10 and Ch 15, the Ash Widow: "Cinders: attack every character, but Hit is 1. Targets: every standing character, the guardian included." Ch 3, Sight: "one attack aimed at anyone misses." Ch 4, Shield: "Take an attack meant for someone near it." Ch 10, Hold: "if it is an attacking move, the guardian is its first target, and Hold is used up." Ch 10's example: "All three are standing, so all three defend."
- **What the table had to decide:** main game, round 1 (R022). Mira asked whether one Sight Surge (2 Magic) makes the *whole* of Cinders miss, which would mean four defence rolls skipped. Again in D2 (June's miss), and in D6b: does Rook's one Shield take the whole move (does Rook then defend once for everyone?), or one character's part, so that Rook defends twice? The book uses "one attack" for Sight and "an attack" for Shield, but never says how many attacks Cinders is. The two readings swing a lot: a 2-Magic Surge cancelling 4–5 attacks, against cancelling 1. It also matters for Hush ("attack two different characters") and for the generic move.
- **Ruling to use tonight:** Cinders is one attack on each standing character. Each defends separately. Shield, Shelter and Sight's miss each stop the attack on one character. A Shielding guardian defends its own attack and the one it took.
- **Suggested fix:** Ch 10 and Ch 15: "Cinders: one attack on every standing character, the guardian included, each at Hit 1. Each defends on their own. Shield, Shelter and Sight's miss each stop only one of these attacks; a guardian who Shields defends twice." Add a line to the move-table notes: "A move that attacks two or more characters makes one attack on each." The quick reference too.

## 5. What worked

- **The roll and the costs.** Four newcomers had the roll by scene 2. Leaving a Power off to turn an Exposure cost into Hurt came up in scene 1 (Quinn's choice) and in D7 (Marco keeping a public round quiet), and everyone understood it.
- **The opening isn't a scene (ruling 93).** All four players wanted to act during the opening. "Let them; she's gone; that's your first lead" handled it in under a minute.
- **The leads.** Six written leads, "anyone else is a talking lead", and going back with "something new each time" filled five scenes. Switching from the What and where answer to the Weakness answer at half, counted before the roll, worked every time. The rule that a lead taking the coven to half tells them what and where (R009) landed cleanly.
- **The twist** came out naturally in the Book (R012) and from Winnie (R014), and the Ash Widow's line in the fight hit home.
- **Life calls mattered.** All four were answered (scenes 1, 3, 5 and 5), and they drove the biggest decision of the night: staying for scene 5. Chapter 15's Life calls fit the story; Quinn's class trip put her at the fireworks.
- **Potions were fully answered by the book.** Brewing, the limit, choosing kinds, drinking at home (no action, one a scene), naming before the roll and losing it on a Failure (D4), one per attack and only the attacker's own (D4), a Down witch can't use one but can be healed (D3), and never on the threat's turn (D3b, D4). Ruling 94 settled every question at once.
- **The public showdown's innocent rules.** Freeing with Nerve, only a Success, the call's Carry (no action, no roll, casting) taking her out of reach, and "a Broken threat lets go". No questions.
- **Exposure in a public fight.** Once per round, decided at the end of the round. The call and potions count as casting. The Unmaking counts even when it fails (98), and a try that works ends the episode first. D7 showed the pressure properly: one quiet-round try left the coven a failed roll from Seen and a Loss.
- **Ties rolled (92), Sight's miss while Down (100), Shelter on the guardian (99):** each settled with no argument.
- **Down witches still matter.** They help, they join the Unmaking, and a Healing brings them back.
- **The numbers checked out:** Chapter 9's lookup row (4 players, no guardian), Chapter 15's tables, the "13 dice" claim (the biggest roll tonight was 10), and Chapter 7's 86% / 36% Unmaking odds (85.5% and 36.3% worked out exactly). ## 6. How it felt

- **Was losing a real risk?** In the investigation, only through choices. The dice were kind (13 of 15 lead rolls worked, including a Critical), and all 15 Clues came by scene 4. The real risk came from the table's own decision to stay one more scene. In the fight, yes, but briefly. Round 1's two attacks (a hit on Quinn, then Cinders on everyone) put Quinn on 3 of 4 before anyone moved, and Hannah had to be freed on a roll of about 81%. The drills show how fast it goes wrong: two witches Down in one threat's turn (D3b, D2), and one failed Unmaking turning a Win into a Loss (D7).
- **Were Magic and potions spent?** Yes, nearly all of it. The coven had 24 Magic in all (5 each, plus 4 Lives) and ended with 1 (Tess). Rook's call was used for its best purpose: carrying the freed innocent.
- **Was there a dominant tactic?**
  1. **Flame's Surge plus a Blasting potion with the Weakness:** 7 damage on a Success and 8 on a Critical. That is over half of a four-witch Standard threat (12) in one action. With one more good hit, the threat Breaks in round 1 and attacks only once. It is the obvious opener for any coven with Marco. Strong, not unbeatable: on a Partial it is still 6 damage, but it needs 2 Magic and the potion.
  2. **The Book researcher who also brews.** June researched three times and brewed once: two rolls for one action, with no Exposure ever. Test B saw the same. Not broken, because of the potion limit, but it is clearly every coven's best action at home.
  3. **The call's Carry straight after freeing.** No action, no Ward, only casting, and the innocent's danger is gone. With no guardian player it is always right, and the book says so.
  
  Nothing felt unbeatable. The table didn't find a free spend or a skipped cost.
- **How long did it take?** 32 minutes of setup, then about 2 hours 5 minutes of play: opening 5, five scenes at about 88 (including a 7-minute debate before scene 5), showdown 24 (6 of them teaching), closing 12. That is about 2 hours 40 minutes in all, inside Chapter 9's 2–3 hours, with a short fight (Chapter 9 expects about 3 rounds at Standard; this took 2).
- **Did each player get real choices?**
  - *Investigation:* yes, for all four. Quinn chose between Voice and the slow way. June chose between research, brewing and Surging, and Tess between searching, Hide and her Life. Theo's Marco (Wits 1) had the thinnest choices, but talking leads with his Life and brewing gave him a job every scene. He also rolled the scene's Critical.
  - *Keeping magic secret:* weak in the investigation. Exposure peaked at 1 of 4. With a Power, a city lead is 4–6 dice against 2, so only 1 of 7 casting leads had a cost, and Tess's Hide wiped it. In the public fight, round 1 became a casting round before the coven acted (defending with Sight and Veil), so the table cast freely. D7 is where secrecy really bit. In short: Exposure only bites in a long public fight or when Seen is close.
  - *Fighting:* yes. Free first or attack first, carry or guard, which potion on which attack, when to Surge, and whether to try the Unmaking now or wait. Nerve 1 witches had little to choose on defence (Issue 11).
  - *Everyday life:* yes. The Lives were the night's biggest lever. The −1 Magic penalty (and "keep 2 Magic") talked three newcomers into playing a fifth scene with all the Clues in hand, which brought the fight to the fireworks. That is the everyday side of the game having real weight. Whether it's meant to be strong enough to pull a table into the public place with nothing else to gain is worth a look. Life dice came up on only 2 of 15 investigation rolls, below Chapter 9's "about one roll in three".

## 7. Clean or not

**Not clean.** No blocker. But the table had to make up a ruling on showdown flow: whether the Ash Widow's signature move is one attack or one attack on each character, which decides what Sight's miss, Shield and Hold do (Issue 1). It came up in the main game (R022, where it decided whether June spent her Surge) and in drills D2 and D6. Everything else the table needed on core flow was in the book, including all of rulings 88–105. Issues 2–14 are minor wording gaps or design notes.
