# Book Test D: rowdy table, a generated episode at Hard

- **Rules tested:** the rulebook source, v0.9 test edition: `book/src/chapters/*.html` (Chapters 1–8, 9–12 including Chapter 11's generator, 10's threats, 13's coven, and the sheets and quick reference), with the rulings logged in `book/REVIEW.md` up to ruling 105. Nothing else was used as a rule. Chapter 16 was read only to keep the new episode's story from contradicting Low Tide. Where the book is silent, the table made the ruling a table would most likely make, and it is logged as an issue (section 5).
- **Date:** 2026-10-30 to 31.
- **Table:** an experienced Storyteller and 5 experienced players: four ready-made witches from Chapter 13 and one player as Rook.
- **Episode:** rolled with Chapter 11's generator and built by its steps, played at **Hard** as a one-off, from the opening to the closing scene, following Chapter 9.
- **Brief:** a clever, rowdy table. They split up, take risks, use every ability, argue rules, and look hard for dominant tactics, free spends, skipped rolls and ways to dodge costs.
- **Dice:** every die came from a logged dice script (computer random numbers, one d6 per die), with an ID (R001…). 146 rolls in all: 6 for the generator, 33 in the episode and 107 in the drills. The full list is in section 8. Nothing was invented. Where a drill needed a 6 on the move table, the die was re-rolled until it showed 6. Every re-roll is in the list, marked "re-rolled until a 6" (44 discarded re-rolls).

**Result: a Win.** The coven found 18 of 20 Clues in four scenes and went to the Old Sea Baths at Doom 4, two Clues short. Then two Lures in round 1 put Rook on 4 of 5 Hurt and took Tess's action. June's Critical study found the Weakness. The Drowned Choir was Broken in round 2, and a single ten-dice Unmaking in round 3 got exactly the 4 successes it needed. Every witch ended on 0 Magic and Rook on 0 Ward. Exposure never left 0.

**Clean? No.** There is one blocker (Issue 1: two Lures on the same witch in one round), and it came up twice, in the main fight and in drill D5. The table also had to invent core-flow rulings in the main fight (Issues 1 and 6) and in the drills (Issues 2, 3 and 5).

### The story
In the great storm of 1824 the sea took Old Saltby and its church, St Agnes, and the drowned still sing. In the winter of 1956 a nine-year-old girl, Iris Pell, was lost all night in the fog on Saltmarsh. The choir sang to her under the ground and kept her from walking into the sea, and she sang back until dawn. They loved her voice. She promised to come back, and she never did. Now she is 79, her voice is thin, and the choir wants it before it goes. With her voice it will feed and grow strong enough to sing a whole street into the sea. It is helped by Amos Fenn, the drowned sexton of St Agnes. He failed to ring the warning bell in 1824, and now he walks up out of the water at night to gather singers for the choir.

### The opening
Rehearsal night in the Great Hall on University Hill. The chamber choir is practising for Iris Pell's farewell concert on Saturday. In the middle of a carol, Sorcha Kenny, nineteen, stops singing. Then she opens her mouth again, and an old hymn comes out, in a voice that is many voices at once. She walks out of the hall still singing, down the hill and across the Harbour road, and goes down the weedy steps of the Old Sea Baths into the rising tide. A fish-market porter pulls her out at the last moment. On the floor of the Great Hall, from the door to the place where she stood, runs a line of wet, bare footprints that nobody made.

### The innocent
**Iris Pell**, 79, retired head of music at the university, who still leads its chamber choir. **What she wants this week:** to get through her farewell concert without her hands shaking, and to tell her granddaughter Ellie the story nobody believed. **Why the threat wants her:** she promised to come back and sing with them.

### The leads (Chapter 11: two talking, two searching, one Book, one Reveal)
1. **Talking (Heart): Iris Pell**, in her room in the music building. She tells of the fog in 1956, the singing under the ground and her promise. All week she has heard the hymn in the hall's organ pipes. Quinn's Life (teacher; Hillside School's choir is the guest act at her concert) fits.
2. **Talking (Heart): Sorcha Kenny and Den Callow**, the porter who pulled her out. Sorcha hums the hymn without knowing it and remembers a wet man putting a sheet of music in her coat. Den saw a man in a dripping oilskin with an unlit lantern walk down the Sea Baths steps and not come up. Den rides Tess's night bus, so her Life fits.
3. **Searching (Wits): the Great Hall and choir room.** Wet footprints from the stage door to the risers. Damp hymn sheets in an 1820s hand, "St Agnes, Saltby", in six singers' folders. Seawater in the organ pipes. June's Life (student, after-hours access) fits.
4. **Searching (Wits): the Old Sea Baths slipway at low tide.** Bare wet footprints come up out of the pool and go towards University Hill, and none go back. "SALTBY" is scratched again and again in the old changing cubicles.
5. **Book research: the attic.** A page by Hester Lane, a witch of the house who helped search the marsh in 1956: "The Saltby singers kept the Pell child all night and gave her back at dawn. The drowned do not forget a voice they have loved."
6. **Reveal: the footprints on the slipway.** Magic shows a drowned man in an oilskin leading a line of sleepwalkers down the steps, and, under the black water of the pool, rows of singers standing in the drained basin.

### What the Clues tell
- **Early:** people who sing in Larkhaven are humming an old hymn and walking into the sea at the Old Sea Baths. Someone wet is leaving hymn sheets for singers. It all points at Iris's choir.
- **At half (10):** it is the Drowned Choir of Old Saltby. It has loved Iris since the fog of 1956 and wants her voice, and it hides under the water of the Old Sea Baths, where it can be met at low tide.
- **All 20 (the Weakness):** a bell from the drowned church, rung at low tide. The small brass handbell on Iris's piano, which was in her hand when the searchers found her in 1956, is St Agnes's own.

### As Doom rises
- **Doom 1:** a busker is found at the bottom of the Sea Baths steps, wet to the waist, humming.
- **Doom 2 (a new victim):** Tomasz Wren, the choir's tenor, walks into the sea. The lifeboat crew needs three tries to pull him out, and he wakes with no voice.
- **Doom 3:** wet footprints come up the hill to the Lantern House and stop on the step, with an unlit lantern left beside them.
- **Doom 4 (a move on the innocent):** Iris is found at 3 a.m. in a garden in her nightdress, singing, with the gate open.
- **Doom 5:** the tide turns on the night of the concert, and the choir comes to the Great Hall.

### The places
- **Hidden place: the Old Sea Baths**, below the harbour wall. A Victorian tidal pool with changing cubicles cut into the rock, closed since 1974 and covered by the sea at high tide. At low tide it drains to a basin of black water, and the choir stands in it. **Ways in and out:** the iron steps down from the promenade (gate chained), and the old storm-drain tunnel from the fish market.
- **Public place: the Great Hall on University Hill**, during Iris's farewell concert: 400 people, the choir on risers, the organ. **Ways in and out:** the main doors to the quad, and the stage door to the loading bay.

### The twist (2: a second, weaker threat is helping)
Amos Fenn, the drowned sexton: story only, with no stats, and he never fights. He leaves the footprints and the hymn sheets. **It comes out** with the Reveal lead, or at Doom 3 if nobody has followed it by then.

### Life calls (a d6 row for every Life, as Chapter 11 asks)

### The closing
- **Win:** the bell rings across the Old Sea Baths, the water goes still, and the singing stops. On Saturday Iris conducts her farewell concert. In the last hymn the hall hears more voices than are on the stage, and then only the choir. The Drowned Choir gets a page in the Book.
- **Partial:** if it fled, the tide comes in over the baths and the choir sinks back, still hungry, and Iris keeps the bell by her bed. If Iris was lost, the hall is empty but for wet footprints from her conductor's stand to the door.
- **Loss:** if Doom filled, Iris walks down the Sea Baths steps singing and is never found, and the next low tide is louder (plan 4: when it returns, +2 Toughness on top of the usual +2). If the coven retreated or every witch went Down, the choir sinks back to try again, and if Iris was in the fight, it takes her.

### Building it
The six rolls took two minutes. Turning them into the notes above took the Storyteller about 45 minutes, against Chapter 11's "half an hour of notes". Most of the time went on three things. First, a backstory that lets 200-year-old ghosts have "once loved" someone alive. Second, the Choir's Weakness, which names its own drowned church, so the bell had to be moved into this story (Issue 20). Third, making a story-only helper give the leads something to find. The rolled threat matched Low Tide's, but the new innocent, places and plan made it feel like a different night.

## 4. Drills (fresh rolls; each starts from the state given)

### D1. Lure with Hold, Shield and Shelter in play (rulings 63, 64, 82, 85, 99)
**Setup:** the Great Hall (public), Hard, round 2, Weakness known (Defence 2), Toughness 6/16, Exposure 1/4. Rook carried Iris out in round 1, so a 6 means Lure. Marco 1/4 (4 Magic), Quinn 0/4 (4), Tess 2/4 (3), June 2/4 (3), Rook 1/5 (3 Ward).
- **Rook Holds.** R040: Success.
- **Femi wanted Tess to Shelter Rook,** so that the Held attack would hit a guardian who can't be hurt. Ruling 99 says a Sheltered guardian "can't Shield or Hold", but it doesn't say what happens to a Hold already waiting. **The table ruled that Shelter ends a waiting Hold** (that combo is what ruling 99 was written to stop) (Issue 3). So Tess Shelters June instead (Nerve 1, the most fragile). **R041: Partial**: it works, at 1 Hurt (Tess 3/4).
- **Marco attacks.** R042: Success, 3 damage (9/16). Marco hurt it last.
- **Quinn casts Hide** (Craft 2 + Voice 2). R043: Success, Exposure 1 → 0. June holds her action.
- **Threat, attack 1:** R044–R046 re-rolled, **R047: 6, Lure.** Under Hold, the attack goes to Rook, and Hold is used up. Rook defends with Nerve 2 + 1 Ward: **R048: Failure**, Hit 3 (Rook 4/5). **The Heart roll still happens:** Tess, Heart 1 + 1 Magic: **R049: Partial**, so she keeps her action.
- **Attack 2, R050: 3,** whoever hurt it last: Marco. Shield and Sight's miss could both be used, and Shield is offered first. Rook (4/5) declines (Issue 11). June Surges (2 Magic), and the attack misses. That isn't a lost attack.
- **End of round:** casting this round (Shelter, Flame and Sight; Hide doesn't count), so Exposure 0 → 1. Doom +1.

**D1b. Hold and a 6 with Iris free and guarded** (ruling 85). Iris is free and Quinn guards her, Rook 0/5. **Rook Holds.** R051: Partial, it works at 1 Hurt (1/5). R052–R055 re-rolled, **R056: 6**: Iris is free, so the threat goes for her. That's an attack on her guard, Quinn, and Hold sends it to Rook (Hold used up). The 6 went to the innocent, so there's no Lure and no Heart roll. **R057:** Rook's Nerve 2 defence fails, Hit 3 (4/5). Quinn and Iris are untouched.

**D1c. Hold and a 6 with Iris free and unguarded.** Rook 0/5. **R058:** Hold works at 1 Hurt. R059–R071 re-rolled, **R072: 6**: an unguarded, free Iris is **taken and lost**. That isn't an attack, so **Hold stays.** **Attack 2, R073: 6** again. Iris is gone, so this time it's **Lure**: its attack goes to Rook under the waiting Hold. **R074:** Rook's defence fails, Hit 3 (4/5). **R075:** Tess's Heart roll fails, so she loses her next action. All as rulings 64, 82 and 85 say.

**D1d. Lure aimed at a Sheltered witch.** **Quinn Shelters Tess** (3/4). R076: Partial, it works at 1 Hurt to Quinn. R077–R099 re-rolled, **R100: 6, Lure.** It's aimed at the most Hurt, Tess, who is Sheltered: no roll and no Hurt. **The Heart roll still falls on Tess:** Heart 1 + 2 Magic, **R101: Partial**, so she keeps her action. Shelter stops the attack but not the Heart roll, exactly as Chapter 10 says.

### D2. Hush, with the Quiet Man swapped in (ruling 89)
**Setup:** a hidden place, Hard, round 2, Weakness known, the Quiet Man at 13/16. Marco 1/4, Quinn 0/4, Tess 2/4, June 1/4, Rook 0/5 (3 Ward).
- **Rook Holds.** R102: Failure, 1 Hurt (1/5) and no Hold.
- **Threat:** R103 re-rolled, **R104: 6, Hush.** It attacks the most Hurt, Tess (2), then the next most Hurt: Marco, June and Rook are tied on 1, **R105: 4, June.** "First" follows that order.
- **Tess defends** with Nerve 3 + Veil 2 (Hush doesn't touch defence dice). **R106: Partial**, 1 Hurt (3/4). She is the first one hurt, so **Tess is Hushed.**
- **Rook Shields June** (1 Ward): **R107: Failure**, Hit 3 (Rook 4/5). Rook isn't Hushed, because only the first one hurt is.
- *Argument (Issue 2):* if Rook had been the first one hurt, could it Hold on its next action? Hush says "can't use a Power, cast a spell or use a gift". Hold is a gift, but "isn't casting", and ruling 89 says Hush stops the next action "being casting". **Ruling: a Hushed guardian can Hold** (the move's own headline is "can't cast"). Shield takes no action, so it isn't stopped either.
- **Round 3:** Tess can't use a potion while Hushed. **Quinn uses a Healing potion on her** instead (Tess 3 → 1). ## 5. Issues

Blockers first. Each has the chapter and exact words, what the table had to decide, a one-line ruling for tonight, and a suggested fix.

### Blocker

**1. Two Lures on the same witch in one round: is the second lost action wasted, or next round's?**
- **Where:** Ch 10, Lure: "on a Failure they lose their next action… The lost action is this round’s if they haven’t acted yet, or else next round’s." Ch 10 and Ch 7 on attacks, for comparison: "It loses at most one attack a round… any more are wasted."
- **What the table had to decide:** main fight, round 1 (R028–R031), and again in D5 (R123–R124). Two 6s came up, so there were two Lures. Both Heart rolls fall on the same witch (the lowest Heart, Tess), and with Heart 1 she can't reach a Partial without spending Magic. After the first Failure, she has lost this round's action but hasn't "acted yet". Read literally, the second Lure also takes "this round's" action, so it is wasted. Read by "lose their next action", it takes next round's. The answer decides whether Tess acts in round 2, and it had to be known **before** her roll, because it decided whether she spent 2 Magic. With 4 or 5 characters the Choir makes 2 attacks a round, so two 6s in one round come up about once in every 36 rounds, and the second Heart roll almost always lands on the same Heart 1 witch.
- **Ruling to use tonight:** the second Failure takes her next action that isn't already lost (next round's).
- **Suggested fix:** Ch 10 and Ch 16, Lure, and the quick reference: "If this round's action is already lost, they lose next round's." (Or, if Richard prefers, "A character loses at most one action a round to Lure; any more are wasted.") Either way, say it.

## 7. How it felt

- **Was losing a real risk?** In the investigation, no: 5, 4, 5 and 4 Clues a scene, and the dice were kind (two Criticals). In the fight, a little. In round 1 Rook was the centre of it: both Lure attacks, Ward on each defence, and a Hold. In round 2 Rook had nothing useful to do: a second Hold doesn't stack, there was no Ward to spare for Shield or Mend, and the guardian can't attack. In round 3 its last Ward helped the Unmaking. Rook took 3 Hurt in the fight (2 from Lure's attacks, 1 from Hold), was healed for 2, and kept every witch standing, which felt important but passive. The guardian's night depends on arriving rested, and Rook arrived on 2 of 5 after choosing leads over rest.
- **Does the generator make a playable episode?** Yes. It took longer than the book says, and it needed invention where the rolls rubbed. A 200-year-old ghost choir that "once loved" a living woman needed a 1956 backstory. The Choir's own Weakness pointed at a church that wasn't in this story (Issue 20). The helper twist added colour and a good argument but nothing to play against. Repeats are common with six-row tables: the first roll matched Low Tide's threat, and the second (D7) almost reproduced Chapter 11's own worked example.
