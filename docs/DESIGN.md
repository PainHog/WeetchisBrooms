# Witch & Ward (working title): design document

This is a living document. **§1 Pitch** is Richard's own. **§3 Decisions** lists what Richard has approved. Since 2026-10-30 that includes choices made under his standing rule: pick the recommended option and log it (he can overrule any of them). Everything in §4–§6 is undecided until Richard chooses.

## 1. Pitch (Richard Moore, 2026-10-30)

The pitch gave no name. Richard chose *Witch & Ward* after the name check (§2), in the follow-up below.

> **Game name:** Witch & Ward (working title)
>
> **Concept:** New Game
>
> Charmed Themed game, its based on the original show. we would have to refrain from using any IP material like darklighter, whitelighter, the manors name, etc. You would play a group of charmed individuals, one player could play a whitelighter for a total of 4 players and the ST. We can add demons maybe, or other things from the show that could be fun, but its not important right now. The idea is to take the concept of the show and morph it into a new TTRPG with that as the conceptual basis.
>
> **Follow-up, after the name check:** well what if we allowed more witches adn we went weith something like Witch & Ward. instead of just the power of 3

### What the pitch settles
- **A new tabletop RPG built on the concept of the original *Charmed* TV show,** not a game of the show: the show is the conceptual basis only.
- **No material from the show.** The pitch names darklighters, whitelighters and the manor's name as examples ("etc.").
- **The players are a group of witches** ("charmed individuals"), and **more witches are allowed** than the show's three.
- **One player could play the group's guardian** (the role the show calls a whitelighter). The pitch says "could", so it's an option.
- **Four players and a Storyteller** in the first pitch. Whether "more witches" changes that count is open (§5, question 1).
- **Demons, or other ideas from the show, may come in later.** They're "not important right now".

## 2. Name check (2026-10-30; risk flags, not legal advice)

- **No game or product called "Witch & Ward" or "Witch and Ward" turned up.** The searches covered the exact title with RPG, tabletop, board game and card game, plus DriveThruRPG, itch.io, BoardGameGeek, Kickstarter, Steam, Amazon and trademark listings. **The name looks clear so far.**
  - **Near misses:** *Ward*, a trading card game; *Ward 3e*, a small indie RPG on itch.io; and novels such as *The Ward Witch* (Sarah Painter) and the *Wards and Wands* series.
  - **In Richard's own games,** *Nightwake* has an enemy called a Warden. Keep "Ward" meaning something different here.
- **Never use "Charmed"** in the title, on the cover, in store text, or as a term in the game.
  - It's a registered US trademark: a word mark since 1998, renewed in 2020, and another registered in 2018.
  - The show already has a licensed board game, *Charmed: The Book of Shadows* (Tilsit; English edition from Clash of Arms). No licensed *Charmed* RPG turned up.
- **The show's own material to stay clear of** (ideas are free; names, terms and distinctive designs aren't):
  - **Terms:** Charmed Ones, the Power of Three, whitelighter, darklighter, orbing, the Elders, the Source (of All Evil), and the manor and family names.
  - **Characters:** the sisters and their guardian, and every other named character.
  - **Designs:** the spellbook marked with a triquetra, the show's house, its logo lettering, its theme song and its spell rhymes.
  - **"Book of Shadows"** is a real Wiccan term from the 1940s–50s, not the show's. It's so tied to the show, though, that a different name for the group's book is safer.
  - **Free to use:** witch families, inherited powers, guardian spirits, demons, spellbooks, potions and protecting innocent people. These are common to the genre.
- **The closest existing games:**
  - *Monster of the Week* (Evil Hat): episodic monster hunting in the style of TV shows, with *Buffy* as an influence.
  - *CJ Carella's WitchCraft* (Eden Studios, Unisystem): gifted magicians against supernatural threats.
  - *Buffy the Vampire Slayer Roleplaying Game* (Eden Studios): a licensed TV-show RPG, the nearest in format.
  - *Hexen: Witch, Please*: comedy-horror witches on a simple d6 system.
  - *Coven & Crucible: Reforged*: modern witchcraft and covens.
  - *Wights* and *Cupcake Witches* (Steffie de Vaan): small games about covens fighting demons.

  **What sets this game apart:** a bonded group of witches that **can grow beyond three**, a **guardian played by a player**, and witch life alongside the demon of the week. Make those the centrepiece.
- **Run a full search before any store page, cover or logo:** DriveThruRPG, itch.io, Steam, Amazon toys and games, the USPTO and the UK IPO.

## 4. Early flags: where the pitch meets the lessons in `DESIGN-RULES.md`
These aren't decisions. They're the first things to settle, because each one has already caused trouble in an earlier game.

1. **Identity: this sits closest to *Nightwake* and *Second Nature*.**
   - ***Nightwake*:** ordinary people woken to powers, fighting supernatural enemies.
   - ***Second Nature*:** young people with a secret power and an everyday life, a hidden enemy, and a campaign of missions.
   - **Decide early what makes this game different:** witchcraft itself (spells, potions, a shared book), family and home, a guardian at the table, and protecting innocent people.
   - **What it can reuse:** *Nightwake*'s fight against a Guard, where a miss pays a resource back (for demon fights); *Second Nature*'s two sides of a character, with its everyday life and its power; and the group spends and help rules every game shares.
   - **Names Richard's books already use** (see the studio's game notes): the Hunt, Clash, Harm, Awakening, Bonded, Warden and Priya. Don't reuse them here.
2. **Staying clear of the show while keeping its idea** (A16; Part D, "no famous-character echoes" and the trademark search).
   - The pitch's own words, "charmed individuals" and "whitelighter", are the show's. **The game needs its own word for everything:** the witches, the group, the guardian, the guardian's enemy, the guardians' masters, the house and the book.
   - Keep a glossary from day one (A16) and check each new term against §2 and against Richard's other games.
   - **Decide what the game keeps from the show's feel:** the setting and era (the late 1990s, or today), the city, and the mix of drama, romance, comedy and horror.
3. **The guardian is a different kind of character** (A4, A6, A8, A9, A21).
   - **A guardian that's simply better** at healing, moving and knowing things makes the witches' own options pointless (A6, A9). One that's weaker means nobody picks it.
   - **Instant travel or healing are the classic free roll-skippers** (A8): *Heisty Spideys*' Phase Through and *Don't Get Forked*'s Ghost both skipped rolls this way. Healing must not undo the threat cheaply either (A3).
   - **Decide** what the guardian does that a witch can't; what it can't do; whether it rolls; what happens if nobody plays one, or two people want to; and what threatens it.
4. **"More witches, not just three": the group's strength must work at every size** (A9, A10, A21).
   - The show's core is a three-way bond. If the group's shared power grows with numbers, a small group suffers; if it doesn't, a big group finds it easy.
   - *Nine Lives Gang*'s "three or five players do about as well" was false. *Nightwake* and *Second Nature* make the same claim without numbers to back it.
   - **Decide** the player range; whether the guardian counts as one of the group; per-size adjustments, tested in the simulator; and what happens when a witch is absent or out, and what a newcomer inherits (A10).
5. **Spells and potions** (A5, A7, A8, A14).
   - **Open-ended spells** (writing your own, the show's rhymes) can solve anything for free. Each spell needs a cost, a roll or a limit (A5, A8).
   - **Ingredients, charges and potions** must not run out without a rule (A14), and must actually get spent (A7).
   - **Decide** whether spells are free-form, a list, or a mix; what they cost; and what the group's book does in play.
6. **Demons and the threat** (A1–A3, A22, A24, A25). The pitch says demons "maybe".
   - **Every demon or threat needs a procedure** (d6 tables, not a script), and stats for how it's found, fought, beaten or driven off (A25).
   - **Every track gets every state defined** (A1), with one event, one rise (A2) and no cheap way to undo it (A3).
   - **Fights must be capped or clocked** (A24). *Nightwake* needed a long-fight clock.
   - **Any clock must bind** (A22); *Don't Get Forked*'s dawn arrived in under 1% of raids.
   - **Decide** whether any secrecy or exposure track exists, since magic around ordinary people is a natural second pressure. That's a question, not a decision.
7. **Losing: hurt, out, taken or killed** (A4, A10, A18).
   - Witches fighting demons raises whether characters can die. If they can, **decide what the player does next.** *Heisty Spideys*' rule that nobody sits out is the model to beat.
   - **Losing must cost something but never be better than fighting on** (A4). Say the true cost where players will read it (A18).
8. **Episodes and seasons** (A12, A22, A27, A29).
   - A TV-style game suggests **one-session episodes inside a longer campaign** ("seasons"). Decide which, or both.
   - **The end of an episode must read correctly at the very start and the very end** (A12).
   - **First night under 30 minutes,** with ready-made witches and a ready-made guardian (A27).
   - **The Storyteller's round fits on one card** (A29).
9. **Tone, ages and sensitivity.**
   - **Ages:** Richard's games run from 8+ to 13+. The show mixed romance and real horror; decide the line.
   - **Real witchcraft and Wicca are living religions.** Don't mock real practice, and don't use real symbols (the pentacle, say) as shorthand for evil.
   - **Demons, angels and heavenly councils** touch real faiths. Keep them the game's own, with no caricature of any religion.
   - **Romance** between characters, or with the guardian, is a choice to make, not a default.

## 5. Open questions for the design interview (DESIGN-RULES Part B, matched to this game)
1. **Identity:**
   - **Format:** tone (the drama, comedy and horror mix), ages, player count now that more witches are allowed, and session length.
   - **Shape:** one-off episodes, a campaign of seasons, or both.
   - **Setting:** the era (the late 1990s or today), and a real city or an invented one.
   - **The title:** keep *Witch & Ward*, or adjust it.
   - **Links to Richard's other games:** none in a series so far; *Nightwake* and *Second Nature* are the closest (flag 1).
2. **The witches and their guardian:**
   - how many witches, and what binds them (family, a coven, chosen by fate);
   - where their shared strength comes from, and how it works at every group size (flag 4);
   - what the guardian does that a witch can't, what happens if nobody plays one, and whether it counts as one of the group (flag 3).
3. **Engine.** Reuse one of Richard's proven engines or build new, with the trade-offs for witches, spells and demon fights:
   - *Heisty Spideys*' d6 success pool, with Partials and a spend-only resource;
   - *Don't Get Forked*'s trait die plus the Mask or Monster risk die, a risk choice on every roll (good for magic that can go wrong);
   - *Nine Lives Gang*'s highest-die pool with a push;
   - *Nightwake*'s fight against a Guard, where a miss costs Vigour but pays Chi (good for demon fights);
   - *Second Nature*'s d6 pool with two sets of ratings (an everyday self and a witch self);
   - or a new engine.
4. **The witches:**
   - creation steps, with random tables for every step and duplicate rerolls;
   - their powers: inherited or chosen, active or passive, how they grow;
   - their everyday life: work, family, relationships;
   - ready-made witches, and what a replacement inherits.
5. **Magic:** spells (free-form, a list, or both), potions and ingredients, the group's book, costs, limits, and what can't be done by magic (flag 5).
6. **The guardian:** its powers and limits, whether it rolls, healing and travel without skipping rolls, its own threat or weakness, and its masters, if any (flag 3).
7. **Threats:**
   - demons and any other enemies, with stats or procedures;
   - how they're found, fought, and beaten or driven off;
   - any enemy that hunts guardians;
   - innocent people: who they are and what happens to them.
8. **Time:** the shape of an episode, and what a turn, round and scene each are. What one action is, whether moving costs it, group actions, and helping (who, how many, what it costs).
9. **Threat tracks and clocks:** every track and every state, one event, one rise, a clock that binds, and any secrecy or exposure track (flag 6).
10. **Resources:** what's earned and spent (spell power, potions, anything the guardian uses), and checking that none is ever free or dominated (A5–A7).
11. **Failure:** hurt, out, captured, possessed or killed; coming back; what everyone-down means; whether you can give up; and what an out player does (flag 7).
12. **The end:** every way an episode ends, read literally at its first and last moment; result tiers; rewards; and what carries over between episodes.
13. **Party size:** every supported count, with and without a guardian player, with explicit adjustments.
14. **Ready-made content:**
    - episodes with maps that match their text room by room, with two or more ways in and out;
    - a roster of demons;
    - the group's home base;
    - an episode or demon generator, if any, made of validated tables (A25–A26).
15. **The first night:** dice, sheets, ready-made witches and guardian, a threat tracker with enough rows, and a round card (A27–A29).
16. **Simulator targets:**
    - episode win and loss rates by difficulty and group size, with and without a guardian player;
    - demon-fight length;
    - how often witches are taken out;
    - spell and resource use;
    - balance between powers, and between the guardian and another witch.
17. **Art direction** (style; each witch's and the guardian's look; staying clear of the show) and **Foundry** (from day one, or book first?).

## 6. Suggestions (for discussion, not decisions)
- **Make the group the star.** For example, a shared spell that every witch can join, each paying part of the cost, so it gets stronger with more hands but never free. Scaling to any group size is then built in.
- **Give the guardian a support spotlight.** For example, it acts between the witches' turns, heals and carries people, and senses danger, but never defeats a demon on its own, and it has its own enemy to fear.
- **Two halves of a witch's life.** Like *Second Nature*'s two forms: an everyday complication each episode (a job, a date, family) that pulls against the demon hunt.
- **Spell-writing as a table moment.** An optional party rule: a player who says a short rhyming spell aloud earns a small bonus, inside the bonus cap. Fun, but it must stay fair and optional.
- **Episodes shaped like TV.** A fixed episode order (an opening scare, the investigation, the book, the showdown, a closing scene) with a one-page episode generator.
